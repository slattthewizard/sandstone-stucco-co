## Is hail damage covered?

The Insurance Bureau of Canada (IBC) said in July 2020: "Damage caused by hail and wind is typically covered by home, commercial and comprehensive auto insurance policies." Typically isn't the same as always, and IBC doesn't name stucco in that statement. Check your own policy, and ask your insurer what applies to your exterior walls and your deductible.

For context, IBC puts the August 2024 Calgary hailstorm at "$3.25 billion" in insured damage in its February 2025 update, and CatIQ's final industry estimate, issued 5 August 2026, is CAD 3.349 billion. IBC said in 2024 that more than 130,000 claims were made.

## The steps IBC gives after hail

IBC's guidance after the August 2024 storm, once it is safe to go outside, runs like this:

- Assess and document the damage. Taking photos can be helpful.
- Call your insurance representative or your insurer's claims department to report your damage.
- If possible, assemble proofs of purchase, photos, receipts and warranties.
- Keep damaged items unless they pose a health hazard.
- Keep notes and be as detailed as possible when documenting damage and providing information.
- Keep all receipts related to cleanup.

After you report, IBC says an adjuster is assigned and may ask for a Proof of Loss form. IBC runs a consumer line at 1-844-2ask-IBC (1-844-227-5422) if you have questions about the process.

The City of Calgary adds one line that matters for anyone with exterior damage: "Before you do any repair work on your property, contact your home insurance provider." Call the insurer first, then look for a contractor.

## What an adjuster is, and who may act as one

The Alberta Insurance Act defines an adjuster in s 2(1) as a person who, for compensation, "directly or indirectly solicits the right to negotiate the settlement of a loss under a contract of insurance on behalf of an insured or insurer or a reciprocal insurance exchange," or "negotiates the settlement of a loss under a contract of insurance on behalf of an insured or insurer or a reciprocal insurance exchange."

Two parts of that definition are worth reading slowly. It covers negotiating a settlement, and it also covers soliciting the right to negotiate one. Someone who offers to take over your claim, for pay, is within the wording even if they never speak to your insurer.

Section 460 then sets the licence rule. A business may not act or offer to act as an adjuster unless it holds a valid adjuster's certificate of authority, with a narrow exception for the insurance agent who sold the policy. An individual may not act or offer to act as an adjuster unless one of four conditions is met: they are a licensed adjuster working for a licensed business, a qualifying agent, an insurer's employee, or an attorney for a reciprocal exchange. The Act excludes some people, such as lawyers, from the definition.

So the question to ask anyone who offers to run your claim is simple: are you a licensed adjuster, and who licensed you?

### Licence types you may hear about

The Insurance Agents and Adjusters Regulation sets out level 1, 2 and 3 adjuster certificates. The Alberta Insurance Council, which issues adjuster licences, also lists a Restricted Hail Adjuster licence. The Council's Adjuster Licenses page is the place to look up what each one allows, and to check a name.

You may also hear the term "public adjuster." The Alberta Insurance Act doesn't define it, so the term itself tells you nothing about whether a person is licensed. Ask for the Alberta Insurance Council licence, then check it with the Council.

## Where a stucco contractor fits in

Your insurer assigns an adjuster to your claim. That adjuster decides what the insurer will pay, under your policy. A contractor's role is different: to look at the wall, say what repair is involved, and give you a written estimate.

You're free to give that written estimate to your insurer. It describes the work and the price from the contractor's side. It does not settle the claim, and a contractor who says they'll "deal with the insurance company" or "get your claim approved" is promising something that is not theirs to promise. Alberta law reserves negotiation of a settlement for licensed adjusters and a short list of others.

## Warning signs after a storm

Alberta.ca says "Most problems relating to home renovations involve contractors who solicit contracts door-to-door." Add an open insurance claim to that and the pressure can go up. Watch for:

- someone who says they can get your claim approved, or promise there will be no cost to you
- anyone who asks you to sign over your claim or to let them speak for you with your insurer
- a request to sign a contract before your insurer has seen the damage
- a large deposit up front (see the stucco contract deposit rules in Alberta for what the law says about contracts that are paid before the work is done)
- no written estimate, or a vague one

## What to do this week

1. Photograph the damage once it's safe, and keep notes with dates.
2. Call your insurer's claims department or your representative to report it.
3. Ask what documents they need, and keep every receipt.
4. Hold off on repair work until you've contacted your insurer, as the City advises, and ask whether they want to see the damage first.
5. Ask one or more contractors for a written estimate you can give your insurer.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Who handles my hail damage claim in Alberta?

Your insurer does. After you report the damage, IBC says an adjuster is assigned, and that adjuster may ask for a Proof of Loss form. A contractor can give you a written repair estimate to pass along, but isn't the one who settles the claim.

### Can a contractor negotiate my insurance claim for me?

Not unless the person is a licensed adjuster or fits one of the other categories in s 460 of the Insurance Act. The Act defines an adjuster as someone who, for compensation, negotiates or solicits the right to negotiate a settlement. Ask any person who offers to run your claim for their Alberta Insurance Council licence.

### What is a public adjuster in Alberta?

The Insurance Act doesn't define the term, so it doesn't tell you whether someone is licensed. The Alberta Insurance Council issues adjuster licences, including a Restricted Hail Adjuster licence, and you can check a name with the Council. Licence level and type matter more than the title.

### Should I repair my stucco before the insurer visits?

The City of Calgary advises: "Before you do any repair work on your property, contact your home insurance provider." IBC also advises photographing and documenting the damage and keeping receipts. Call your insurer first and ask what they need before any work starts.
