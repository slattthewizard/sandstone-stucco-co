## Why Calgary keeps coming up in hail stories

The City of Calgary says plainly that Calgary is located in "hail alley", the area that sees the most hail in Canada due to its geography. The same City page says all communities within Calgary are equally exposed to hail. That second line matters. A storm cell hits a particular stretch of the city on a particular day, but over the long run no quadrant is the safe one and no quadrant is the unlucky one. Where the last big storm landed tells you about that storm, not about your street's future.

So when you read below that one storm hit the north and another scraped across the northeast, take it as a description of the weather on that day. It isn't a ranking of neighbourhoods.

## The big storms, one at a time

The table below uses the wording of the sources named. Insured loss figures are what the Insurance Bureau of Canada (IBC), CatIQ and Environment and Climate Change Canada (ECCC) reported, and they're about insurance payouts, not about stucco specifically.

| Date | Insured loss, as reported | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 13 June 2020 | "almost $1.2 billion" at first; about $1.3 billion later, from 63,000 claims minus crop losses | IBC, 8 July 2020; ECCC |
| 5 August 2024 | "nearly $2.8 billion", then "$3.25 billion", then a final CAD 3.349 billion | IBC, Sept 2024 and Feb 2025; CatIQ, 5 Aug 2026 |
| 13 July 2025 | "close to $92 million" | IBC, 14 Aug 2025 |

### 13 June 2020

IBC's first number, published on 8 July 2020, was "almost $1.2 billion", and it described the area hit as Calgary, Airdrie, Rocky View County and the surrounding area. ECCC's later write-up of the year's weather puts the storm at about $1.3 billion across 63,000 claims, not counting crop losses. It describes a hail core that "scraped over northeastern Calgary," and says hail the size of tennis balls and golf balls came down on winds up to 70 km/h. In ECCC's words, the hail "riddled house siding with millions of dents."

A Canadian Meteorological and Oceanographic Society (CMOS) bulletin on the same storm puts the core of the damage in a zone of 10 to 15 square kilometres just east of the Calgary Airport.

### 5 August 2024

This is the storm with the number that kept moving. In September 2024, IBC put the insured damage at "nearly $2.8 billion." By February 2025 IBC's figure had risen to $3.25 billion. On 5 August 2026, two years to the day, CatIQ published its final industry loss estimate of CAD 3.349 billion. CatIQ also estimated that more than 95 percent of claims were resolved by the two-year mark.

IBC said the storm "primarily affected northern parts of the city of Calgary," with more than 130,000 insurance claims and nearly 60,000 homes in the Calgary area impacted. IBC also noted that the same area of north Calgary had been hit by the June 2020 hailstorm. ECCC wrote that the hail reached the size of hen eggs and describes ice chunks "punching holes in vinyl siding and destroying roofs." It described the storm's path as Calgary's northern suburbs and parts of the city's northeastern quadrant. IBC ranked it as the second-costliest event in Canada's history, behind the 2016 Fort McMurray wildfire.

Read those numbers carefully. A claim count and a loss figure that both grew for two years after the event tell you something practical: damage reports kept arriving, and settlements kept being worked out, long after the sky cleared.

### 13 July 2025

The 2025 storm is the small one in the table, but it still counts. IBC reported close to $92 million in insured damage from the 13 July 2025 hailstorm in Calgary. That's a fraction of 2020 or 2024, and it's still a lot of broken material.

### The wider five-year picture

IBC also noted in August 2025 that insured damage from hailstorms across Alberta over the previous five years stood at $6 billion. That figure is for the province, not Calgary alone, and it comes from a single industry source. It does show how much of the bill comes from a handful of days in a handful of summers.

We found no 2026 Calgary hail loss release as of the end of September 2026. That can change at any time, so check IBC's news page for anything newer.

## What this history means for a stucco wall

A fair question is what any of this has to do with your wall. A few things follow from the record without stretching it.

### Hail is a repeat event here, not a one-off

Two storms in four years hit the same stretch of north Calgary, according to IBC. If you're buying, selling or renewing insurance, it's reasonable to ask about the age and condition of exterior walls and roofing, because the storms are on the record.

### Stucco is not untouched by it

The City of Calgary's climate-ready guidance says stucco is fire resistant and will hold up well to flying debris in moderate wind and hail, but that severe storms can cause damage. The City is describing the material in general. It isn't a promise about your wall, and a severe storm is exactly what Calgary has had. For a longer look at how stucco behaves in hail, see [is stucco good in hail](/blog/stucco-hail-resistance/).

### Damage can be small and still count

Martini Stone Masonry, a Calgary contractor, says golf-ball-sized hail leaves 2 to 5 mm indentations that break the moisture barrier. That's one firm's description, not a test result. The practical point is simple: what looks like cosmetic pitting from the driveway may be worth a closer look from someone on a ladder, because water getting behind the wall does its damage out of sight.

### Timing matters more than most owners expect

The claim numbers above show that insurers and adjusters were still sorting out 2024 claims well into 2026. If your wall took a hit, start the paperwork early. IBC's own steps are to assess and document the damage once it's safe, with photos, then call your insurance representative or your insurer's claims department. The City of Calgary adds: before you do any repair work on your property, contact your home insurance provider. For the step-by-step version, read our post on [hail damage to stucco in Calgary](/blog/hail-damage-stucco-calgary/).

### Insurance is a conversation between you and your insurer

IBC says damage caused by hail and wind is typically covered by home, commercial and comprehensive auto insurance policies. "Typically" is the operative word, and stucco isn't named in that statement. Check your own policy and talk to your insurer. ## Looking ahead to the next hail season

Some preparation is boring and still useful.

Photograph every elevation of your house now, in good light, while it's undamaged. Keep a dated set somewhere that isn't on a phone you could lose. IBC's own advice after a storm is to assemble proofs of purchase, photos, receipts and warranties if possible, and a before-and-after set makes the "after" much easier to explain.

IBC also said in June 2026 that a majority of insurers will provide home insurance savings of up to 50% when hail-resistant materials are in place. That's IBC's statement about insurers in general, not an offer or a promise, and whether a given stucco wall or product qualifies is a question for your own insurer. It is a good reason to ask before you choose materials for a repair or a re-stucco job.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Which part of Calgary gets the most hail?

The City of Calgary says all communities within Calgary are equally exposed to hail, and that the city sits in "hail alley." Individual storms follow their own paths. The 2020 and 2024 storms were reported as hitting northern and northeastern areas, but that describes those storms, not future ones.

### How much did the 2024 Calgary hailstorm cost?

IBC first reported nearly $2.8 billion in September 2024, then $3.25 billion in February 2025. CatIQ's final industry loss estimate, published on 5 August 2026, is CAD 3.349 billion. IBC reported more than 130,000 insurance claims.

### Is hail damage to stucco covered by home insurance?

IBC says damage caused by hail and wind is typically covered by home, commercial and comprehensive auto insurance policies, but it doesn't mention stucco. Your own policy decides what's covered. Call your insurer before any repair work, as the City of Calgary advises.

### Will there be another big hailstorm in Calgary?

Nobody can say when. What the record shows is that Calgary has had large events in 2020 and 2024 and a smaller one in 2025, and that the City describes the whole city as equally exposed. Photographs of your walls in good condition and a look at your insurance policy are sensible steps whatever the weather does.
