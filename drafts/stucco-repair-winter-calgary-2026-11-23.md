# Stucco Repair in Winter: What You Can Do Before Spring

It's late November, there is a crack running down the wall by the back door, and you're wondering whether anyone will touch it before April. The honest answer is that cement stucco has temperature limits, the manufacturers publish them, and most of them are well above freezing. Stucco repair in winter is possible in some cases with heat and enclosures, but it isn't the same job as a July repair, and it's worth knowing what to expect.

## What the manufacturers say about cold

Cement stucco cures through a chemical reaction that needs warmth and moisture. Two manufacturers publish minimum temperatures, and both are written into their technical documents.

| Manufacturer and product | What the document says |
|---|---|
| Imasco, traditional stucco guide specification (2024) | No stucco work when ambient air and substrate temperatures are below 4°C (39°F) or above 38°C (100°F) for 24 hours before, during and after application |
| Imasco, cement finish technical data sheet (2024) | Base coat to be applied above 4°C and to stay above 4°C for a minimum of 48 hours |
| ADEX, DRYMIX base coat technical bulletin (2021) | Ambient and surface temperatures kept at or above 5°C (41°F) for a minimum of 24 hours |
| ADEX, CRYSTAL liquid coat technical bulletin (2019) | Ambient and surface temperatures at or above 5°C (41°F) for a minimum of 24 hours |

Imasco is a cement stucco manufacturer based in Surrey, BC. ADEX is another stucco system manufacturer. These are the makers' own instructions for their own products, not a Calgary rule, and the specific product on your wall decides which limit applies.

Imasco's guide specification also says to moist cure cement-based stucco with clean water for a minimum of 48 hours, and not to spray walls with high-pressure water for at least 72 hours. That's worth remembering before anyone suggests rinsing a fresh patch in cold weather.

ADEX also says additives of any kind, including antifreeze and accelerators, are "strictly forbidden and may void warranties" for its base coat. So the shortcut some people imagine, mixing something into the batch to let it set in the cold, is something at least one manufacturer prohibits.

## What winter repair looks like in Calgary

Calgary Parging, a Calgary contractor, says on its stucco repair cost page that stucco cures best above 5°C in dry conditions, typically April through October in Calgary, and that off-season repairs require heated hoarding. Hoarding is a temporary enclosure, usually framed and wrapped, with a heater inside to keep the wall above the manufacturer's minimum. Hoarding and heat cost money, and not every contractor offers winter work at all, so ask whether it's included and how it's priced.

Not every contractor describes winter work the same way, and some talk about cold-weather additives. ADEX forbids additives in its base coat, and the manufacturer minimums in the table above are the figures to hold anyone to. If a contractor tells you a repair can be done in the cold, ask for the product's written temperature limit and how the enclosure will hold the wall above it, including overnight.

Another Calgary firm, Calgary Stucco and Slate, says some repairs take two trips and some take up to four, with up to weeks in between so each layer cures properly. Multiple coats mean multiple stretches of cold weather to get through, which can stretch a winter job out.

## What can wait, and what shouldn't

Not every crack is urgent, and not every crack can wait. These are three hypothetical situations, not customer stories, to show how the thinking goes.

Say there's a hairline crack on a sheltered wall, the same width as it was in the autumn, with no staining below it. Many homeowners would photograph it and plan for spring. A contractor who sees the wall can tell you whether that's reasonable.

Say there's a bulge or a patch that sounds hollow when tapped, and it's on a wall that takes weather. Calgary Stucco and Slate says nobody can tell you the full extent of water damage until the wall is opened up, so a spot that is already letting water in is a reason to call sooner. Weststar Wall Systems, another Calgary firm, lists soft or spongy areas and peeling or bubbling as warning signs.

Say there's a hole at the base of a wall where the stucco has crumbled. Martini Stone Masonry says snow piled against stucco softens and crumbles the lowest 12 to 18 inches of the wall. That's the firm's claim, but it's a fair reason to look at what is piled against yours.

## What you can do in winter

You can't repair the wall yourself in freezing temperatures without running into the same limits the manufacturers set, but there's a lot you can do to protect it and to get ready.

### Document everything

Take clear photos in daylight, wide and close, with a tape measure or coin beside any crack for scale. Note the date. The Insurance Bureau of Canada suggests you assess and document the damage and says taking photos can be helpful. Take the same photos again every few weeks. A crack that hasn't moved is a different thing from one that has.

### Keep snow and water away from the wall

Shovel snow back from the base, and check that downspouts empty well away from the foundation. Calgary Parging says over 30% of its residential repairs address water damage caused by incorrectly installed rain gutters, eaves or flashing, in its own 2024 job mix.

### Don't seal it with whatever is in the garage

Caulk or paint over a failing area can hide the problem and trap water behind it. Don't pressure wash stucco in the cold either. Weststar warns that high-pressure water can damage stucco and force moisture inside.

### Call your insurer first if hail is involved

The City of Calgary says: "Before you do any repair work on your property, contact your home insurance provider." The Insurance Bureau of Canada says hail damage is typically covered by home insurance policies, so check your own policy. This site does not handle, file or negotiate claims. A contractor can give you a written repair estimate that you may pass to your insurer, and the claim is between you and your insurer. Our page on [hail damage to stucco](/blog/hail-damage-stucco-calgary/) covers the damage itself.

### Check your warranty

If your home is newer, check your new home warranty before paying for repairs. The New Home Buyer Protection Act applies to homes built under a building permit applied for on or after 1 February 2014, and whether a given problem counts as a defect is for the warranty provider. Hail damage isn't a construction defect, so don't count on the warranty for it.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can stucco be repaired in freezing weather?

Manufacturers set minimum temperatures that are above freezing. Imasco says no stucco work below 4°C for 24 hours before, during and after application, and ADEX calls for at or above 5°C for at least 24 hours for its base coat. Calgary Parging says off-season repairs require heated hoarding, so winter work means an enclosure and heat, or waiting for spring.

### What is heated hoarding?

It's a temporary enclosure built around the work area, with a heater inside to keep the wall at a workable temperature. Calgary Parging says off-season repairs require it. Hoarding and heat add to a job, so ask any contractor to show the cost as its own line on the quote.

### Is it safe to leave a stucco crack until spring?

It depends on the crack and the wall. A hairline crack that hasn't changed may be a spring job, while a bulge, soft area or a spot where water is getting in is worth showing to a contractor sooner. Photograph it, watch it, and ask a contractor to look.

### Can I use an additive to repair stucco in the cold?

ADEX says additives, including antifreeze and accelerators, are strictly forbidden in its base coat and may void warranties. Other products have their own instructions. Ask the contractor which product they plan to use and request its written cold-weather limits.
