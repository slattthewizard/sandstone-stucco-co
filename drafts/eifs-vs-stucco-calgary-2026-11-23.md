# EIFS vs Stucco in Calgary: How to Tell Which Wall You Have

Plenty of Calgary houses look like they have stucco and don't. The wall can have the same texture, the same colour and the same crack lines as a cement stucco wall, and still be a different system underneath. That matters the day you need a repair, because the two are repaired, priced and sometimes damaged in different ways.

## What EIFS is, and what conventional stucco is

EIFS stands for exterior insulation and finish system. The idea is a layer of foam insulation on the outside of the wall, covered with a thin reinforced base coat and a coloured finish. Martini Stone Masonry, a Calgary contractor, gives a sense of what that looks like in one of its job descriptions: a siding tear-off to an EIFS system with a membrane, "1.5-inch foam," an ADEX basecoat, a mesh called Graphex, primer and an ADEX finish. That is one firm's description of one job, and other systems and thicknesses exist.

Conventional stucco is different. In the traditional three-coat system, cement-based coats go on over lath: a scratch coat, a brown coat, and a finish. Imasco, a cement stucco manufacturer, specifies a scratch coat to a minimum thickness of 10 mm and a brown coat to a maximum of 10 mm, bringing the combined system thickness to 19 mm. So conventional stucco is a hard, cement-based layer with real thickness to it, where EIFS puts foam under a thin coating. Our [three-coat stucco](/blog/three-coat-stucco/) page walks through those layers.

Martini says it works on three kinds of system in Calgary, which it names as "conventional three-coat, IMASCO or EIFS." Calgary Parging, another Calgary contractor, says that cement stucco with an acrylic finish was the main product on over 87% of the residential properties in its own 2024 job mix, and that the top brands it installs are Adex, Sto and Dryvit. That's one firm's own job list, not a survey of Calgary homes, so it doesn't tell you what's on your street.

The word "stucco" is often used for all of these, which is why a quote or a listing may call an EIFS wall stucco. It's worth asking.

## How to tell which one you have

You can't be sure from the street. A contractor can tell you after looking, and your home's original documents may say. A few things to look for:

- Tap the wall gently with a knuckle. Cement stucco tends to sound solid. A softer, hollow sound may suggest foam behind the finish, but that's a rough clue, not a test.
- Look at a damaged spot. A hole or chip that shows a white or grey foam layer under a thin coating suggests EIFS. A hole that shows thick grey cement coats and wire or lath suggests conventional stucco.
- Check your records. The builder's drawings, a past inspection report or a renovation invoice may name the system.
- Ask a contractor. A written quote should say which system they found.

If you can only photograph one thing, photograph any existing hole, chip or edge, with a tape measure beside it. Send it through the contact form and the contractor has something to go on.

## Why woodpeckers are drawn to EIFS

This is where the difference can show up in Calgary. Calgary Parging says woodpeckers are drawn to EIFS because "the foam insulation behind the finish is much softer than concrete, and tapping on it produces the hollow resonance" birds associate with insects. The same firm says flickers "work Calgary stucco hard, especially on west and south elevations in spring." Both are the firm's own observations. Martini lists a separate price for "woodpecker damage (EIFS)" at $250 to $350 per hole, depending on height and access, which suggests the firm sees enough of it to price it separately.

Flickers are protected under federal law. The Migratory Birds Convention Act, 1994 lists flickers and woodpeckers as protected, and the Migratory Birds Regulations, 2022 say a person must not capture, kill, take, injure or harass a migratory bird, or disturb an egg, without a permit. So don't harm a bird or disturb a hole with eggs or chicks in it. Non-harmful scare devices, and covering an area with mesh, are options Environment and Climate Change Canada describes for nests, and a damaged hole can be repaired after nesting ends. ## The "deflect versus keep out" question

Calgary Parging says stucco "is designed to deflect water, not to be waterproof," and that the layer that actually keeps water out of the home is the building paper behind it. That's the firm's claim, but it's a useful way to think about both systems. A crack in the finish doesn't automatically mean water is inside the wall, and a tidy finish doesn't prove the layers behind it are dry. We don't state any code requirement for a drainage cavity here, because we haven't found a Calgary or Alberta source that does.

The City of Calgary says stucco does not handle moisture well, and that moisture and snow can cause stucco to shrink and crack. It also says to have qualified professionals inspect the weather-resistive barrier when replacing siding. For either system, the practical point is the same: find out where water is coming from before you cover the symptom. Calgary Stucco and Slate says, "Nobody can tell you the full extent of water damage until the wall is opened up," and Calgary Parging says over 30% of its residential repairs, in its own 2024 jobs, address water damage caused by incorrectly installed gutters, eaves or flashing. ## EIFS vs stucco: points to weigh

Instead of a verdict, here's a short list of questions that tend to decide a quote.

- What's on the wall now? A repair has to match the system underneath, and a contractor will often want to see it before pricing.
- Is there damage you can see? A woodpecker hole, a bulge or a stained patch each points to a different job.
- Is moisture involved? Calgary Parging lists water-damaged sheathing as something to replace first, adding $575 to $2,300 or more to a repair, before GST.
- Would a full re-clad make sense? If damage is widespread or moisture is extensive, Total Exteriors suggests replacement may be the answer. Our [re-stucco in Calgary](/re-stucco-calgary/) page covers that route.

A change to a different cladding material needs a building permit according to the City of Calgary. Like-for-like repair generally doesn't, but the City advises confirming your own project by calling 311. If your home was built under a permit applied for on or after 1 February 2014, check your new home warranty before paying for repairs, since whether a given problem counts as a defect is for the warranty provider to decide.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is EIFS the same as stucco?

No. EIFS has foam insulation under a thin coated finish, while conventional stucco is built from cement-based coats over lath. Martini Stone Masonry, a Calgary contractor, lists "conventional three-coat, IMASCO or EIFS" as separate systems it works on. People often call both stucco, so ask a contractor which one you have.

### Why do woodpeckers peck EIFS?

Calgary Parging says woodpeckers are drawn to EIFS because the foam behind the finish is much softer than concrete, and tapping it makes a hollow sound that birds associate with insects. That is the firm's own explanation. Flickers are federally protected, so don't harm a bird or disturb a hole with eggs or chicks in it.

### How much does EIFS repair cost in Calgary?

Prices vary by job. Martini Stone Masonry lists woodpecker damage on EIFS at $250 to $350 per hole, and Calgary Parging lists a typical woodpecker patch with reinforcement at $600 to $800. Calgary Parging also lists EIFS removal and make-safe for about 1,400 sq ft at $62,100 before GST. These are single firms' published figures, not quotes.

### Do I need a permit to repair EIFS or stucco?

The City of Calgary says most repair or replacement work does not need a development or building permit, and that replacing with a similar material is allowed. Changing to a different siding material needs a building permit. The City page doesn't name EIFS or stucco, so call 311 to confirm before work starts.
