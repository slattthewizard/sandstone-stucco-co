# Acrylic Stucco vs Cement Stucco Finish in Calgary

When you get a stucco quote, the line that says "acrylic finish" or "cement finish" can look like a small detail. It isn't. It's the outermost coat of the wall, the one you see, and it affects how a repair matches, how long you wait to apply it, and what the job costs.

## What "acrylic stucco" actually means

Most people say "acrylic stucco" to mean a wall with an acrylic finish coat. The coats underneath are usually still cement. Calgary Parging, a Calgary contractor, describes the main product on its own jobs as "cement stucco with an acrylic finish." So the choice you're making in most quotes is about the top coat, not about throwing out the cement stucco underneath it.

A cement finish is the traditional version: a cement-based coat, tinted and textured, that goes over the base coats. Imasco, a cement stucco manufacturer, makes finishes of that kind. An acrylic finish uses acrylic polymers as the binder instead, and it comes as a ready-mixed coat in a pail. Martini Stone Masonry, a Calgary contractor, lists an "acrylic bucket" in its price rows, which gives you a sense of how it's supplied.

## What Calgary contractors say about acrylic

Two Calgary firms say things about acrylic that are worth knowing. Both are contractor claims, not independent test results, and we're repeating them with their names attached.

Martini says an acrylic finish "stays flexible through freeze-thaw, holds colour without repainting and resists hairline cracking better than cement finish." That's an appealing list, but it's the firm's own statement and we haven't seen independent testing behind it. Under Alberta's Consumer Protection Act, a supplier's claims about the performance or length of life of goods or services need adequate independent testing behind them, so treat any contractor's claim of that kind as something to ask about, not something to rely on.

Calgary Parging says, going by its own 2024 job mix, that cement stucco with an acrylic finish is the main product on over 87% of the residential properties it works on. That's one firm's mix of jobs, not a survey of Calgary houses, and a firm that installs a certain system is likely to see mostly that system. It does tell you that if your house has an acrylic finish, you're not unusual among that firm's customers. The firm names Adex Systems, Sto and Dryvit as the top brands it installs.

Both firms are also contractors who quote and install these systems, so ask for the reasoning behind a recommendation. A written quote that names the finish, the brand and the number of coats is easier to compare than one that says "stucco repair."

## How the two finishes age differently

This is the practical point that most affects a repair. Calgary Parging says "a cement finish and an acrylic finish age and weather differently." On heavily weathered or discontinued finishes, it says it will tell you up front if a perfect match isn't achievable, and will offer the option of refinishing a full elevation instead of "chasing an invisible patch."

Martini, for its part, says "matching an aged finish takes trial batches." And Imasco says that colour variations may occur due to weather, texture, job conditions and method of application, that you should buy the same colour batch number for the complete job, and that adding water while trowelling will disturb the colour. Its technical data sheet adds that variations are more noticeable in darker colours.

Put those together and you get a sensible picture. A new finish next to a 15-year-old one of a different type will not look the same on day one, and may look different again after a few seasons. We take colour matching further on our [stucco colour matching](/blog/stucco-colour-matching/) page. For this page, the point is that the finish type belongs in the conversation before the patch goes on.

## Curing: why the finish type affects your timeline

Finishes have different timing rules, and the manufacturers publish them. These are manufacturer specifications, not a contractor's opinion.

- Imasco's cement finish data sheet (September 2024) says the finish coat "may be applied after a minimum of 7 days, during ideal conditions," and that the base coat should be applied above 4°C and remain above 4°C for a minimum of 48 hours.
- Imasco's traditional stucco specification says to perform no stucco work when air and substrate temperatures are below 4°C or above 38°C for 24 hours before, during and after application. It also says to moist cure for a minimum of 48 hours and not to spray walls with high-pressure water for at least 72 hours.
- ADEX, a manufacturer of acrylic-based systems, says its DRYMIX base coat needs ambient and surface temperatures at or above 5°C for at least 24 hours, and forbids additives such as antifreeze or accelerators, which it says may void warranties.
- ADEX's CRYSTAL liquid coat bulletin (August 2019) says all cement-based substrates must have a mandatory 1-month curing process before the liquid coat is applied. This is a liquid acrylic coat applied over a finish, not ADEX's trowelled acrylic finish itself, so it isn't a rule for every acrylic job. It does show that an acrylic product can have a longer wait on a cement base than a cement finish does.

So a wall with a cement finish may be ready for its top coat in about a week under ideal conditions, while a liquid acrylic coat over cement may need a month. Which one applies to your job depends on the product the contractor uses. Ask the contractor to name the product and show you its technical data sheet.

Cold is a separate limit. Calgary Parging says stucco cures best above 5°C in dry conditions, typically April through October in Calgary, and that off-season repairs require heated hoarding. That applies to either finish. For more on timing, see our [stucco repair in Calgary](/stucco-repair-calgary/) page.

## Lifespan claims, and what to do with them

HomeStars says acrylic stucco "typically lasts about 25 years." Calgary Parging says properly applied parging lasts 15 to 25 years. Total Exteriors, a Calgary firm, puts the natural lifespan of stucco overall at 40 to 50 years. Those numbers don't line up, because they come from different firms making different claims about different things. None of them is a guarantee for your wall.

What they do show is that nobody has a single agreed figure. Drainage, exposure, workmanship and maintenance all play in. Ask your contractor what they stand behind in writing, and what the contract says about workmanship.

## Three example situations

These are hypothetical, not customer stories.

Say a south wall has a faded cement finish and a few cracks, and the owner wants it refreshed. A contractor may suggest repairing the cracks and refinishing the whole elevation, so the colour reads as one surface. Martini lists one elevation with an acrylic finish at $14,350 before GST, but the real number will depend on the wall.

Say a bungalow had a repair done last year in a slightly different shade. Calgary Parging notes that cement and acrylic finishes age differently, so a mismatch that looks small now can widen. A contractor may suggest re-doing the elevation.

Say a homeowner wants to switch from cement to acrylic during a larger repair. Ask the contractor whether the base coats are staying, how long they need to cure before the finish goes on, and which product they'll use.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is acrylic stucco different from regular stucco?

In most Calgary quotes, "acrylic stucco" means cement stucco base coats with an acrylic finish on top. Calgary Parging says that combination is the main product on over 87% of the residential properties it works on, though that's its own job mix and not a survey. Ask your contractor which finish and brand they plan to use.

### Does acrylic crack less than a cement finish?

Martini Stone Masonry says an acrylic finish resists hairline cracking better than a cement finish and stays flexible through freeze-thaw. That's the firm's claim, and we haven't seen independent testing behind it. Imasco says some cracking is inherent to portland cement, so ask how each option is expected to behave on your wall.

### Can I put an acrylic finish over an existing cement finish?

That depends on the existing wall and the product, so it's a question for the contractor. ADEX's CRYSTAL liquid coat bulletin says all cement-based substrates must have a mandatory 1-month curing process before it is applied. Ask for the product's data sheet and a written timeline.

### Will an acrylic repair match my older cement finish?

Calgary Parging says cement and acrylic finishes age and weather differently, and that on heavily weathered finishes it will say up front if a perfect match isn't achievable. It may offer to refinish the full elevation instead. Ask for that option to be priced separately.
