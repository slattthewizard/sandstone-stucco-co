# Stucco Leaking? Check the Gutters, Eaves and Flashing First

When water shows up on the inside of a wall, or a stain creeps down the outside of the stucco, the natural reaction is to blame the stucco. Sometimes that's right. A Calgary contractor says, from its own repair records, that a large share of the water damage it sees starts somewhere else entirely: gutters, eaves and flashing. This page explains what that means, how the wall is supposed to keep water out, and what to do next.

## How a stucco wall is meant to handle water

It's easy to assume stucco is a waterproof shell. Calgary Parging, a Calgary contractor, says it isn't: "Stucco is designed to deflect water, not to be waterproof. The layer that actually keeps water out of your home is the building paper behind it." That's the firm's description, but it explains why the details at the edges matter so much.

If the cement coat sheds most of the water and the paper behind it deals with whatever gets through, then trouble starts where water arrives in volume, or where it's pushed behind the paper. A gutter that overflows onto a wall delivers a lot of water to one spot. A flashing that's missing or badly lapped gives water a path behind the stucco and the paper. A roof edge that sheds water onto the wall face does it every time it rains.

The City of Calgary's climate-ready page on exterior walls says to have qualified professionals inspect the weather-resistive barriers when replacing siding. It doesn't say what the barrier must be, and we're not aware of a source that states a specific drainage or rainscreen requirement for stucco in Calgary, so this page doesn't claim one.

## What one Calgary contractor says about where the leaks come from

Calgary Parging says that over 30% of its residential repairs address water damage caused by incorrectly installed rain gutters, eaves or flashing. The figure comes from its own jobs in 2024, as the firm published it that year.

Treat that carefully. It describes one firm's workload, not every house in Calgary, and a firm that does a lot of repair work sees houses that are already in trouble. It also can't tell you whether your leak is one of them. Still, it supports the sensible order of questions: before you assume the stucco is the problem, look at what's above and beside it.

## Where water typically gets in

A few places on a house deserve the first look.

### Where a roof meets a wall

A roof edge or a lower roof running into an upper wall is a junction where water collects and flashing has to do real work. Calgary Parging lists roof and deck adjacent repairs at $1,000 to $4,000 in a trends post dated 9 April 2024, so the firm prices these junctions as their own category of repair.

### Decks and balconies

Martini Stone Masonry, a Calgary contractor, lists a deck wall above a garage at $2,400 before GST, describing the job as removing stucco and rotten wood, replacing studs, and rebuilding with plywood, primer, a membrane, wire mesh, two coats of fibre mortar and an acrylic finish matched to the old one. That's one firm's example of what a rebuilt deck wall involves, and it shows where wet wood ends up when water has been getting in for a while.

### Windows and doors

Martini says temperature movement opens 3 to 8 mm gaps where stucco meets window and door frames. A gap that wide at a joint lets water through whether or not the gutters work. We cover that in [stucco cracks around windows](/blog/stucco-cracks-around-windows/).

### Under gutters and downspouts

Where a downspout empties onto the wall or at its foot, the base of the wall takes the load. A blocked or loose gutter can spill water over the edge, down the face of the wall, every time it rains.

None of these is a rule about your house. They're the usual suspects, and a contractor on site is the person to say which one applies.

## Signs that water is already behind the wall

Stains and cracks are the ones people notice. Weststar Wall Systems, a Calgary-area contractor, lists "soft or spongy areas" and "peeling or bubbling" among the warning signs of stucco damage. Others to watch for are a bulge in the wall, a hollow sound when you tap a patch, staining that runs down from a gutter, a roof edge or a window corner, a musty smell, or paint on the inside of the wall that blisters.

Our guide to [stucco water damage signs](/blog/stucco-water-damage-signs/) walks through those in more detail, so we won't repeat it here. The key point is that a stain on the surface tells you where water came out, not necessarily where it got in.

## What hidden water damage does to a quote

This is where the cost question gets honest. Calgary Stucco and Slate, a Calgary contractor, says that nobody can tell you the full extent of water damage until the wall is opened up. A first look can give a range and a plan. It can't promise a final number.

Calgary Parging publishes a line that shows how a quote can move. In its stucco repair cost guide, reviewed in May 2026 and priced before GST (5%), the firm says water-damaged sheathing or board must be replaced first, and to add $575 to $2,300 or more. Its same guide lists bulge repair, for example above a garage or on walls, at $1,450 to $3,000, and repairs around windows or doors at $975 to $3,000.

Dynamic Painting Inc., another Calgary firm, published on 4 April 2026 that moisture-related or larger corrective work ranges from $3,500 to $8,500 or more. It doesn't state whether GST is included.

These are published figures from individual firms, based on their own jobs, and not a Calgary average. Another contractor may price the same work quite differently. For how these pieces fit together, see our [stucco repair cost guide](/stucco-repair-cost-calgary/).

What you can do is ask each contractor the same questions in writing. What will you open up to look at the sheathing? What happens to the price if you find rot? Who repairs the gutter or flashing that caused it?

## Who fixes what

This is the part that trips people up. Stucco repair and roof, gutter or eavestrough work are different trades. A stucco contractor can repair a stucco wall, and can often tell you where the water seems to be coming from. If a gutter is installed wrong or a roof edge is shedding water onto the wall, somebody still has to fix that, and it's usually a separate contractor.

The City of Calgary's storm-damage page says that most repair or replacement work doesn't need a development or building permit. It doesn't list eavestroughs or downspouts as needing one. For your specific job, confirm with the City by calling 311.

Two practical points. If you suspect a recent storm or an insured event caused the damage, the City of Calgary advises you to contact your home insurance provider before doing any repair work. And if your home is newer, check your new home warranty before paying for repairs. The New Home Buyer Protection Act applies to homes built under a building permit applied for on or after 1 February 2014, and whether a particular leak is a covered defect is for the warranty provider to decide.

Fixing the stucco without fixing the cause tends to repeat the problem. That's worth saying out loud to any contractor you talk to. A patch over an active leak is a short-term answer at best.

## Before you call anyone

A few minutes with your phone helps.

1. Photograph the stain or damage from a distance and up close, with a coin or tape measure beside it for scale.
2. Photograph the gutter, the roof edge and the downspout above it.
3. Note when you see it: during rain, during a chinook, after snow melts, or all the time.
4. Don't caulk or paint over it. That can hide the problem and trap water.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can a gutter problem make stucco leak?

It can, according to Calgary Parging, which says over 30% of its residential repairs address water damage caused by incorrectly installed rain gutters, eaves or flashing. That's from its own 2024 jobs. A contractor can look at whether your wall damage lines up with a gutter, roof edge or flashing above it.

### Is stucco waterproof?

Calgary Parging says stucco is designed to deflect water, not to be waterproof, and that the building paper behind it is the layer that keeps water out. That's the firm's description. The practical point is that details such as flashing and gaps at windows matter a great deal.

### How much does it cost to fix water damage behind stucco?

Costs vary by job and by firm. Calgary Parging says water-damaged sheathing must be replaced first and adds $575 to $2,300 or more, before GST, and Dynamic Painting Inc. lists moisture-related or larger corrective work at $3,500 to $8,500 or more. Calgary Stucco and Slate says the full extent can't be known until the wall is opened.

### Will the stucco contractor fix my gutter too?

Not necessarily. Gutter, eavestrough and roof work is a different trade from stucco, so ask each contractor in writing what's included and what isn't. If the gutter caused the leak, it needs to be fixed as well as the wall.
