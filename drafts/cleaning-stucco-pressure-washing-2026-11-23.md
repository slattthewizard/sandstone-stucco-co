## What stucco makers and Calgary firms say

Weststar Wall Systems, a Calgary firm, puts it in one sentence: "High-pressure water can damage stucco and force moisture inside." That's the core of the problem. A pressure washer doesn't just lift dirt off the surface. It can push water into hairline cracks, joints at windows and any spot where the coating has lost its bond.

Imasco, a cement stucco manufacturer, makes a related point from the application side. Its guide specification for traditional stucco says not to spray walls with high-pressure water for at least 72 hours after application. That rule is about fresh work, but it shows the maker treats high-pressure water as something a cement coat needs protecting from.

Then there's the idea behind how a stucco wall is meant to work. Calgary Parging says stucco is designed to deflect water, not to be waterproof, and that the layer that actually keeps water out of the house is the building paper behind it. That's the firm's own claim, but it explains why forcing water into the stucco layer is a poor trade. Once water gets behind the coating, the paper and everything behind it has to cope.

None of this means a wall can never be washed. It means the method matters. The City of Calgary also says stucco does not handle moisture well, and that moisture and snow can cause it to shrink and crack. A wall that is already cracked is the last wall you want to soak under pressure.

## Check the wall before you clean it

Spend ten minutes looking first. Walk the wall in daylight and note anything that isn't just dirt:

- Cracks, especially ones that are wider than a hairline or run from window corners.
- Gaps where the stucco meets frames, or sealant that has pulled away.
- Spots that sound hollow when you tap them lightly with a knuckle.
- Soft or spongy areas, or peeling and bubbling, which Weststar lists as warning signs.
- Stains that run down from a gutter, eave or flashing edge.

If you find any of these, cleaning isn't the first step. Calgary Parging says over 30% of its residential repairs, going by its own 2024 jobs, address water damage caused by incorrectly installed rain gutters, eaves or flashing, so a dark streak under an eave may be telling you where water is going. Photograph anything you find, with a coin or tape measure beside it, before you wash. Washing can change how a crack looks, and a before photo is useful if a contractor looks at it later.

## A gentle way to clean stucco

This is general good practice, not advice from any contractor or manufacturer named on this page, and you should follow any instructions that came with your own stucco system or from your contractor. The idea is simple: use as little force as will do the job.

1. Start by dry brushing. Loose dust, cobwebs and dried-on dirt often come off with a soft-bristle broom or brush, with no water at all.
2. Wet the wall with a garden hose on a gentle setting. Start in a small area and keep the spray soft and pointing slightly downward, not up into joints or under trim.
3. Scrub with a soft brush and plain water. A soft, long-handled brush covers more wall with less effort. Stiff wire brushes scratch the finish.
4. Rinse from the top down so dirty water doesn't stain the clean part below.
5. Let it dry fully, then look at it again in daylight before deciding whether it needs a second pass.

Always test a small, out-of-the-way patch first, and wait for it to dry before you judge the result. Stucco often looks darker when wet and lighter when dry, and a patch that looks clean now may look different tomorrow.

Skip anything that blasts. Don't stand close with a pressure nozzle, don't aim spray upward at the underside of eaves or into the gap at a window frame, and don't use a sharp scraping tool on the surface. If the dirt won't come off with a hose and a soft brush, that's a good moment to stop and ask a contractor, not to turn up the pressure.

Take care near cracks and edges. Even a hose can push water into an open gap, so keep the spray away from them and clean those spots by hand with a damp brush.

## White deposits are not always a defect

Some walls show a pale, chalky film that comes back after you wash it. That's often efflorescence, a mineral deposit left on the surface when moisture moves through cement and dries. Imasco states that some cracking and efflorescence are inherent to portland cement and are not a reflection of product quality.

That doesn't mean you can ignore it. A little light bloom after a wet spell can be normal for a cement wall. Heavy or repeated deposits in one spot may point to water moving through that part of the wall, perhaps from a leaking gutter, a downspout emptying against the base or a crack above. So the useful question is where the moisture is coming from, not how to scrub the white off.

Don't paint over it or seal it in to hide it. Covering a damp patch can trap water behind the coating. If the deposit returns in the same place, take photos and have someone look at the source. ## Winter and washing in Calgary

Cleaning a stucco wall is best done in mild, dry weather when the wall can dry out afterward. Water left in the surface layer in freezing weather can freeze, and the City of Calgary counted 117 freeze-thaw cycles in 2022 against a 30-year average of 128, so Calgary walls get plenty of practice at it. A November hose-down is rarely a good idea. Wait for a stretch of warm days in spring.

Snow against the wall is another matter. Martini Stone Masonry, a Calgary contractor, says snow piled against stucco softens and crumbles the lowest 12 to 18 inches of the wall. That's the firm's description, but it's a good reason to clear snow back from the base rather than wash the wall later.

## When cleaning isn't enough

If, after a gentle wash, you can still see cracks, peeling, stains that come back or a patchy finish, the wall needs a contractor's opinion rather than a stronger wash. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can you pressure wash stucco?

Weststar Wall Systems warns that high-pressure water can damage stucco and force moisture inside, and Imasco's guide specification says not to spray walls with high-pressure water for at least 72 hours after application. Most homeowners are better off with a garden hose and a soft brush. If you do use a washer, ask your stucco contractor first.

### How do I clean green or grey streaks off stucco?

Start with the gentlest method: a dry soft brush, then a garden hose and a soft brush with plain water, rinsing from the top down. Test a small area first and let it dry before judging. If the streaks return in the same place, look for the water source, such as a gutter, eave or downspout.

### What are the white deposits on my stucco?

They're often efflorescence, a mineral film left when moisture moves through cement. Imasco says some cracking and efflorescence are inherent to portland cement and not a reflection of product quality. Heavy or repeated deposits in one spot are worth a closer look for a moisture source.

### Should I wash stucco before a repair?

Usually not. Leave the wall as it is, take photos of what you see and let the contractor decide how to prepare it. Washing first can push water into cracks or hide the evidence of where moisture is getting in.
