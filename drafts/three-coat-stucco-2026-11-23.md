# Three Coat Stucco: What It Is and Why Repairs Take Time

You may have heard a contractor call your wall "conventional" or "three-coat" and wondered what that tells you. It's shorthand for how the wall was built: cement plaster applied in stages, each one allowed to set before the next. Knowing that makes a lot of repair quotes, and a lot of repair timelines, easier to follow.

## What three coat stucco is

Three coat stucco is the traditional cement stucco system. The name comes from the layers: a scratch coat, a brown coat and a finish coat, built up one on top of the other. Imasco, a cement stucco manufacturer based in Surrey, BC, publishes a guide specification for what it calls the Traditional Stucco System (revision 05, 22 October 2024), and that's the clearest public description of the thicknesses involved.

Martini Stone Masonry, a Calgary contractor, says the systems it meets on Calgary walls are conventional three-coat, IMASCO and EIFS. That's one firm's own list, but it's a useful map. Conventional three-coat is the cement plaster build described here. EIFS is a different build altogether, with foam insulation under a thin finish, and we've covered it in [EIFS versus stucco in Calgary](/blog/eifs-vs-stucco-calgary/).

## The thicknesses a manufacturer specifies

Here is what Imasco's guide specification says, in its own terms:

| Layer | Imasco guide spec |
|---|---|
| Scratch coat | To a minimum thickness of 10 mm (3/8 inch) |
| Brown coat | To a maximum thickness of 10 mm |
| Combined total system thickness | Brought to 19 mm after the brown coat |

Those are the manufacturer's numbers for its own system, not a rule for every wall in Calgary. Another firm's product, an older house or a repair patch can differ. But they do show the scale. A traditional stucco wall is a solid cement shell nearly two centimetres thick, not a thin skin of paint.

That thickness is part of why the wall is slow to build. Cement has to be applied, left to set and kept in the right condition before the next coat goes on.

## Why each coat has to wait

Imasco's specification says to keep the plaster uniformly moist with clean water for a minimum of 48 hours. It also says not to spray walls with high-pressure water for at least 72 hours. For the finish, Imasco's cement finish data sheet says the finish coat may be applied after a minimum of 7 days, during ideal conditions.

Temperature matters as well. Imasco says to perform no stucco work when ambient air and substrate temperatures are below 4°C or above 38°C for 24 hours before, during and after application. That's a manufacturer's limit on the wall itself, and it doesn't leave much room in a Calgary spring or fall.

Add those together and a full three-coat sequence is measured in days or weeks, not hours. Our article on [how long stucco repair takes](/blog/how-long-does-stucco-repair-take/) goes through the contractor timelines and the weather windows in more detail.

## Why repairs on a three-coat wall take more than one visit

A small crack or chip may only need a patch, and that can be a short job. A larger repair that cuts back to the sheathing means rebuilding the layers, and the layers have to cure between steps.

Calgary Stucco and Slate says some repairs take two trips and some take up to four, with up to weeks in between so each layer cures properly. That's the same logic as the manufacturer's cure times, described from the contractor's side. Martini's cost page describes one of its published jobs, a deck wall above a garage, in similar terms: stucco and rotten wood removed, studs replaced, plywood, primer, a membrane, wire mesh, two coats of fibre mortar and an acrylic finish matched to the wall. Each of those items is a step, and several of them can't start until the one before has set.

What a homeowner sees from the street is a crew arriving, leaving for several days, and returning. That isn't a sign the job has been abandoned. A good contractor will explain the gaps and give you a written sequence, so ask for one.

Two things can lengthen it further. If water has been getting in, Calgary Stucco and Slate says nobody can tell you the full extent of the damage until the wall is opened up, so a quote may come with an allowance for what's behind. And matching an older finish is its own step, as Imasco advises that colour can vary with weather, texture, job conditions and application method. ## How common is it on Calgary houses?

One Calgary firm, Calgary Parging, said in a 2024 post that cement stucco with an acrylic finish continues to be the main stucco product on over 87% of the residential properties it works on. That's the firm's own job mix, not a city survey, so don't read it as a Calgary-wide statistic.

It also shows why "three coat" and "acrylic" aren't opposites. The base can be cement plaster, and the finish on top can be cement or acrylic. ## How to tell what you have

You usually can't tell from the outside. A few clues can help, though none is conclusive:

- The age of the house. Ask what system was used when it was built.
- Any paperwork from the builder or a past repair that names the system.
- A look at a cut edge or old repair, if there's one, where the layers show.
- A contractor's opinion after they've seen the wall.

Don't assume. Some Calgary walls are EIFS, which looks similar from the street but is built differently, and the repair method and cost differ. Calgary Parging, for instance, lists EIFS panel removal and make-safe of about 1,400 sq ft at $62,100 ex GST on its 2026 cost guide, which is a different scale of job from a three-coat patch. A written quote should say which system the contractor believes you have.

## What this means for your quote

Ask the contractor three plain questions. Which system is on the wall? Is the repair a patch of one coat, or does it go back to bare wall and rebuild the layers? How many visits, and how many days apart? The answers explain most of the price and the schedule.

Also ask which product the contractor will use, and whether its technical sheet states cure times and temperature limits. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### What are the three coats in three coat stucco?

They're the scratch coat, the brown coat and the finish coat. Imasco's guide specification gives the scratch coat a minimum thickness of 10 mm and the brown coat a maximum of 10 mm, bringing the combined system to 19 mm.

### Is three coat stucco the same as EIFS?

No. Three coat stucco is a cement plaster system, while EIFS uses foam insulation under a thin finish. Martini Stone Masonry names conventional three-coat, IMASCO and EIFS as separate systems it works with. Our article on EIFS versus stucco covers the differences.

### Why does my repair have several visits?

Cement has to cure between steps. Imasco states a minimum of 48 hours of moist curing and a finish coat after at least 7 days in ideal conditions, and Calgary Stucco and Slate says some repairs need up to four trips with up to weeks between them. Ask your contractor for the sequence in writing.

### Can a three coat wall be patched?

Often yes, for small cracks and chips, but it depends on what's behind the damage. A contractor can tell you on site whether a patch is enough or whether the layers need rebuilding. Hidden water damage can change the scope once the wall is opened.
