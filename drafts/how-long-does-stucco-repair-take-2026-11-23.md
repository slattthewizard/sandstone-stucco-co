# How Long Does Stucco Repair Take? Calgary Timelines Explained

You've got a quote in hand, or you're about to ask for one, and the question every homeowner asks next is simple: how many days will there be a crew and scaffolding at the house? The honest answer is a range. A small patch can be a day or two, and a larger repair can stretch across several visits spread over weeks.

The reason isn't slow workers. Cement-based stucco has to cure between steps, and the weather decides when it can. ## Why a stucco repair can't always be done in one visit

A traditional cement stucco wall is built in coats, and a repair that goes back to bare wall repeats those coats. Our guide to [three-coat stucco](/blog/three-coat-stucco/) walks through the layers. For timing purposes, the point is that cement needs moisture and time to harden properly, and the manufacturers say so.

Imasco, a cement stucco manufacturer, gives these figures in its traditional stucco guide specification (revision of 22 October 2024):

- Keep the plaster uniformly moist with clean water for a minimum of 48 hours.
- Don't spray the wall with high-pressure water for at least 72 hours.
- Apply the finish coat after a minimum of 7 days, in ideal conditions, per its cement finish data sheet.

ADEX, another manufacturer, states that its DRYMIX base coat cures in approximately 24 hours under normal climatic conditions. For a liquid acrylic coat called CRYSTAL, ADEX states that cement-based substrates must go through a mandatory one-month curing process before the coat is applied. That coat sits over a finish rather than being a patch, so it won't apply to every repair, but it shows how much the stated times vary by product.

Put those numbers together and it's easy to see why a job that needs a base coat, a wait, and a finish coat can't be squeezed into an afternoon. A contractor who says "two visits a week apart" may simply be following the product's data sheet. Whether a shortcut is safe is a question to put to the contractor and the manufacturer's literature, not to us.

## What changes the timeline

A few things move a job from the short end to the long end.

Scope is the obvious one. A hairline crack on one side of a house is a very different job from a bulge above a garage or a section that has to be cut out and rebuilt. Calgary Parging's own cost guide separates small patches, medium repairs with two or three areas, bulge repairs and section replacements, and the time follows the same ladder.

Hidden water damage is the second. Calgary Stucco and Slate says nobody can tell you the full extent of water damage until the wall is opened up. If the sheathing behind the stucco is wet or soft, it has to be dealt with first, and Calgary Parging notes that water-damaged sheathing or board must be replaced first. That's work that wasn't on the original schedule.

Height and access come next. Calgary Parging says upper-storey or peaked sections need scaffolding, which it prices at $4,000 to $12,900, ex GST. Putting up and taking down scaffolding is itself a stage of the job.

Colour and texture matching can add a step. Calgary Parging lists a colour-match add-on at $230 to $575, ex GST. Imasco advises that colour can vary with weather, texture, job conditions and application method. A contractor who wants a trial patch to check the match before committing the whole wall is being careful, and it may add a visit. See our article on [stucco colour matching](/blog/stucco-colour-matching/) for more.

Then there's the weather.

## Weather windows in Calgary

Cement doesn't cure well in the cold, and the manufacturers put hard numbers on it. Imasco says to perform no stucco work when ambient air and substrate temperatures are below 4°C or above 38°C for 24 hours before, during and after application. ADEX asks for ambient and surface temperatures at or above 5°C for a minimum of 24 hours for its base coat, and says additives such as antifreeze or accelerators are strictly forbidden and may void warranties.

Calgary Parging says stucco cures best above 5°C in dry conditions, typically April through October in Calgary, and that off-season repairs require heated hoarding. That's one more thing that stretches a winter timeline: the enclosure has to be built, heated and kept heated through the cure.

If you spot damage in November, the realistic plan is often a spring repair, with the area protected until then. Our piece on [stucco repair in winter in Calgary](/blog/stucco-repair-winter-calgary/) covers what that looks like. A contractor who claims a cement repair can go ahead at any temperature should be asked to show you what the product's own technical sheet says.

Rain, snow and strong sun matter too, because a fresh coat has to stay moist but not wash out. Ask whether the timeline allows for weather days, and what happens to your dates if it doesn't.

## How to get a timeline you can use

A single "it takes a few days" isn't enough. Before you sign, ask for these in writing:

- The number of visits expected, and roughly how many days apart.
- What happens if the sheathing is wet and has to be replaced.
- Which product system will be used and what its data sheet says about cure times.
- What the contractor does if the weather stops work partway through.
- When scaffolding goes up and comes down, if it's needed.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How long does a small stucco patch take?

Martini Stone Masonry says minor crack repairs finish in 1 to 2 days, and Calgary Parging says most of its repairs take 1 to 3 days depending on scope and weather. A very small patch may be done in a single visit. If the contractor needs to match a finish, ask whether a second visit is planned.

### How long does stucco take to cure?

It depends on the product. Imasco states a minimum 48 hours of moist curing and a finish coat after at least 7 days in ideal conditions, and ADEX states approximately 24 hours for its DRYMIX base coat under normal conditions. Ask your contractor which product they're using and for its technical data sheet.

### Can I use the wall or pressure wash it right after a repair?

Imasco's guide specification says not to spray walls with high-pressure water for at least 72 hours after application. For anything beyond that, such as painting or sealing, follow what the contractor and the product literature say for your repair.

### Does winter delay stucco repair?

It can. Imasco says no stucco work below 4°C for 24 hours before, during and after application, and ADEX calls for at or above 5°C for at least 24 hours for its base coat. Calgary Parging says off-season repairs require heated hoarding, so winter work costs more time, and a spring repair may be the practical choice.
