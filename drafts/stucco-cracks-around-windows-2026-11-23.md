There's a thin dark line running out from the corner of a window, or a gap along the frame you can slide a fingernail into. It's easy to put off. The wall looks fine from the street, so it waits another winter.

## Why the cracks show up at windows

Stucco is a rigid cement coating. A window frame is vinyl, wood or metal, and it expands and contracts with temperature at its own rate. Where the two touch, there's a joint, and that joint takes the strain every time the weather swings.

Calgary gives that joint a lot of practice. The City of Calgary counted 117 freeze-thaw cycles in 2022 and 54 chinook days, against a 30-year average of 128 cycles and an average of 46 chinook days a year. Those figures are in the City's [2022 climate hazards year in review](https://www.calgary.ca/content/dam/www/uep/esm/documents/climate-hazards-2022-year-in-review.pdf). One year's count isn't a forecast, but it gives you a sense of how often a wall here swings between cold and mild.

Martini Stone Masonry, a Calgary contractor, lists window and door separation as a failure it sees: it says temperature movement opens gaps of 3 to 8 mm where stucco meets frames. That's the firm's own description, and it's worth treating as a contractor's observation, not a measured standard.

There's a second cause that has nothing to do with the window. Calgary Parging says that over 30% of its residential repairs, going by its own 2024 jobs, address water damage caused by incorrectly installed rain gutters, eaves or flashing. If a downspout or an eave dumps water onto the wall above a window, the crack under the window may be the symptom, not the problem. Our article on [gutters, flashing and stucco leaks](/blog/stucco-gutter-flashing-leaks/) goes through where to look.

## Cosmetic crack or open joint?

Not every line at a window corner means water is getting in. Imasco, a cement stucco manufacturer, states that some cracking and efflorescence are inherent to portland cement and are not a reflection of product quality. So a fine hairline running diagonally from a window corner, which is a common place for stress to concentrate, may be just that.

An open joint is different. If there's a visible gap between the stucco and the frame, daylight, a crumbling edge, or a bead of old caulking that has pulled away on one side, rain can run straight into the wall at that point. Wind-driven rain and meltwater from snow on the sill both find that kind of opening.

A few things are worth checking from the ground and from inside:

- Whether the crack is a hairline or has a visible gap you could slide a coin into.
- Whether the old caulking is cracked, shrunken or missing along the frame.
- Whether there are stains, bubbling paint or soft drywall below the window inside the house.
- Whether the sill itself slopes away from the glass or lets water sit.
- Whether the crack is the same size it was last season.

Take a photo with a coin or tape measure beside the crack, note the date, and take the same photo again in a few months. A crack that hasn't moved is a very different thing from one that has. The article on [when stucco cracks are worth worrying about](/blog/stucco-cracks-when-to-worry/) covers widths and patterns across the whole wall.

Weststar Wall Systems, another Calgary firm, lists "soft or spongy areas" and "peeling or bubbling" as warning signs on stucco. If either shows up near a window, treat it as more than a surface problem.

## What a window crack repair involves

Contractors don't all do this the same way, and the scope is something you should expect to see written down. In general the work is one of three things.

The first is resealing. The old caulking or sealant is cut out along the joint, the joint is cleaned and a new sealant goes in. It's the smallest job and it's usually the right one when the stucco itself is sound and only the joint has failed.

The second is a crack and patch repair. The cracked stucco around the opening is opened up, filled and refinished, and the finish is matched as closely as the contractor can manage. Matching is its own challenge. Imasco advises that colour can vary with weather, texture, job conditions and application method, and an older wall will have weathered since it was applied. Our guide to [stucco colour matching](/blog/stucco-colour-matching/) explains why a patch can show.

The third is deeper work. If water has been getting in behind the stucco, the sheathing or framing behind the window may be wet or soft. Calgary Parging says water-damaged sheathing or board has to be replaced first and puts that at an extra $575 to $2,300 or more on its cost guide. Calgary Stucco and Slate notes that nobody can tell you the full extent of water damage until the wall is opened up. That's a fair reason a quote for a window job may come with a caveat.

## Do you need a permit?

Repairing stucco around a window isn't the same as changing the window. The City of Calgary's storm-recovery page says that for most repair or replacement work you won't need a development or building permit, and it doesn't list a permit for replacing a window of the same size. It does list development and building permits for larger or smaller windows or doors. The City's renovation guidance adds that creating new openings in exterior walls needs a permit.

So, in plain terms: resealing or patching around a window, or swapping a window for one of the same size, generally doesn't need a City permit. Making an opening bigger or smaller does. Before you commit, confirm with the City by calling 311 for your own project.

If your home was built under a building permit applied for on or after 1 February 2014, the New Home Buyer Protection Act applies, and a home warranty must cover defects in the building envelope for at least five years from when coverage begins. Whether a particular window leak counts as a defect is for the warranty provider to decide, so check your new home warranty before paying for repairs.

## When to call someone

Call a contractor sooner rather than later if you can see light through a gap, if the sealant is gone, or if there is staining or soft drywall inside the house.

If a recent hailstorm may be involved, the City of Calgary advises contacting your home insurance provider before you do any repair work. The IBC says hail damage is typically covered by home insurance policies, so check your own policy and keep photos.

Winter timing matters too. Calgary Parging says stucco cures best above 5°C in dry conditions, typically April through October in Calgary, and that off-season repairs require heated hoarding. A sealant-only job may be different from a cement repair, so ask the contractor what their product allows. A crack you find in November may be a spring job, with the gap kept shut meanwhile if the contractor says that's safe to do.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why does stucco crack at the corners of windows?

Corners concentrate stress, because the stucco changes direction there and the frame moves at a different rate. Martini Stone Masonry says temperature movement opens 3 to 8 mm gaps where stucco meets frames. A hairline that starts at a corner may be cosmetic, but if the gap is open, have a contractor look at it.

### Can I just fill the gap with caulking myself?

A fresh bead of sealant can be a reasonable short-term measure for a joint that has simply dried out, but it can also trap water if the problem is behind the wall. Don't smear caulking over a crack that's wider than the joint was designed for, or over a bubbled or soft area. A contractor can tell you on site whether a reseal is enough.

### How much does it cost to repair stucco around a window in Calgary?

It depends on whose figures you read. Calgary Parging publishes $975 to $3,000 for repairs around windows and doors (May 2026, ex GST) and $600 to $1,200 per window on a 2024 page. Martini Stone Masonry lists $300 to $800 per opening for resealing. A written quote for your own wall is the only price that counts.

### Do I need a permit to repair stucco around a window?

The City of Calgary says most repair or replacement work doesn't need a development or building permit, and it lists none for a window of the same size. Larger or smaller windows or doors need development and building permits. Call 311 to confirm for your project.
