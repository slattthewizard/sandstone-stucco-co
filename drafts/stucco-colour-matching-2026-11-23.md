# Stucco Colour Matching: Why Patches Show and What to Ask

You can fill a crack in an afternoon and still be looking at it from the driveway for the next ten years. A new patch of stucco often reads as a different colour from the wall around it, even when the repair itself is sound. Stucco colour matching is the part of the job that most often disappoints, and it helps to know why before you agree to a quote.

## Why a stucco patch rarely matches

A wall that has been up for fifteen years has been through fifteen Calgary winters of sun, snow and chinooks. New finish, sitting next to it, hasn't. That is the simplest reason a patch shows, and it's only the first one.

Manufacturers say plainly that colour is hard to hold steady even on a brand new wall. Imasco, a cement stucco maker based in Surrey, BC, states in its colours datasheet that "variations in colour may occur due to weather, texture, job conditions and method of application." So the same bag of finish can dry slightly differently on a cool, damp morning than on a warm, breezy afternoon, and a trowel-finished patch and a sprayed wall won't catch light the same way.

Four things tend to be behind a visible patch:

- The surrounding finish has aged, and the new finish hasn't.
- The patch comes from a different production batch than the original.
- Water was added on the wall while the finish was being worked.
- The texture is a little different, so the surface throws shadows differently.

Texture is easy to underrate. Two finishes can be exactly the same colour in the bucket and still look like two colours on the wall, because a coarser surface casts more small shadows and reads darker.

## What the manufacturers say about batches, water and dark colours

Imasco's published documents give four specific warnings that explain a lot of patch problems. They are written for new work, but they apply to repairs too.

Start with batch numbers. Imasco's colours datasheet says: "It is important to purchase the same colour batch number for the complete job." Its guide specification for the traditional stucco system adds that installers should "avoid application of separate batches of finish side-by-side." A repair on a house that was finished years ago can't use the original batch, which is why nobody can guarantee a perfect blend.

Then there is water. The same datasheet says adding water while floating or trowelling "will disturb the colour." Imasco's technical data sheet for its cement finish says the addition of water will result in a lighter colour than expected. On a hot day it's tempting to loosen a stiff mix on the wall. Doing that to a patch can produce a pale spot that wasn't in the sample.

Dark colours are less forgiving. Imasco's data sheet says variations are more noticeable in darker colours. A deep charcoal or brown wall will show a patch more readily than a pale sand or cream one.

Weather and job conditions matter too. Temperature and humidity during application and curing affect how the finish dries. This is one more reason a repair done in a different season than the original can look different. Our article on [stucco repair in winter](/blog/stucco-repair-winter-calgary/) covers the temperature limits that keep cement products from being applied in the cold.

None of this means a good match is impossible. It means a contractor who promises an invisible patch on a weathered wall is promising something the manufacturers themselves don't promise.

## Cement and acrylic finishes age differently

Many Calgary houses have an acrylic finish over a cement base. Calgary Parging, a Calgary contractor, says on its stucco repair page that a cement finish and an acrylic finish age and weather differently. That matters if your wall is cement-finished and the repair material is acrylic, or the reverse. Even two acrylic finishes from different years may have shifted apart.

Martini Stone Masonry, another Calgary firm, says acrylic "stays flexible through freeze-thaw, holds colour without repainting and resists hairline cracking better than cement finish." That's the firm's own claim about its product, so ask any contractor what they'd use and why. The practical point for you is to find out which finish your wall already has before the quote is written, and to say so on the quote.

## Trial batches and the honest conversation

Martini states that "matching an aged finish takes trial batches." That sentence is worth holding on to. It describes a process of mixing small samples, letting them dry, and comparing them to the wall in daylight, because wet finish looks different from dry. A contractor who does this will take more time than one who picks a colour from a sample card, and that time may show up in the price.

Calgary Parging goes a step further. Its stucco repair page says that on heavily weathered or discontinued finishes the firm will say upfront if a perfect match isn't achievable, and will offer the option of refinishing a full elevation instead of chasing an invisible patch. That's the firm's stated approach, not a rule every contractor follows. But it's a reasonable thing to ask for from any contractor you talk to: tell me before you start if you can't match this, and price the alternative.

## Patch it or refinish the whole elevation

An elevation is one full side of the house. Refinishing a whole elevation means the colour change, if there is one, falls at a corner where the eye expects a break, not in the middle of a wall.

Say a south wall has had two small repairs near a window, and the finish is faded by sun. A patch might be matched to the faded colour, but the next repair will be matched to the patch, and so on. Refinishing the wall in one go would reset it, at a higher cost.

Or say the damage is a single woodpecker hole high on a west wall, partly hidden under an eave. The cost of refinishing the whole elevation may not be worth it, and a small patch that is a little off in colour could be a fair trade.

Or say a section of wall has been cut out and rebuilt after moisture damage. A rebuilt section is large enough that mismatch is obvious. Ask for two prices in writing, one for the repair with a colour match and one for refinishing the elevation, and compare them with the photos in front of you.

These are hypothetical examples, not customer stories, and what is right for your wall depends on what a contractor sees on site.

## What to ask a contractor about matching

You don't need to be an expert to ask good questions. These cover most of it.

1. Which finish is on my wall now, cement or acrylic, and what will you use for the repair?
2. Will you mix trial samples and let me see them dry against the wall before you apply?
3. If the original colour or batch is no longer available, how will you handle it?
4. If you can't get a close match, will you tell me before starting, and what's your price for refinishing the full elevation?
5. Is the colour match priced as its own line, and does the quote show GST separately?
6. What temperature and weather conditions will you work in?

Then get the answers in writing. Alberta.ca advises homeowners to insist on a written contract and to get written estimates from more than one business. Its [hiring a contractor page](https://www.alberta.ca/hiring-contractor) also says to make sure the contractor and tradespeople have Workers' Compensation Board coverage. The City of Calgary says contractors need a valid City of Calgary business licence, and you can call 311 to confirm one.

## Photograph before you call

Take pictures in daylight, on an overcast day if you can, since direct sun flattens colour differences. Photograph the whole wall, the repair area up close, and a corner so the contractor can see how the elevation breaks. If you have a leftover can or bucket from the original job, or a paint code written down somewhere, mention it. It can help, though a contractor may still want to take a sample from the wall.

If hail or another insured event caused the damage, the City of Calgary advises contacting your home insurance provider before you do any repair work. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can stucco be matched exactly?

Not always, and manufacturers don't claim it can. Imasco says colour can vary with weather, texture, job conditions and method of application, and recommends the same batch number for a complete job. On an older wall, a close match is the realistic goal, and some firms offer full-elevation refinishing when a patch won't blend.

### Why does my new stucco patch look lighter than the rest of the wall?

There are a few common reasons. The old finish has aged and darkened or faded, the patch is from a different batch, or water was added during application. Imasco's data sheet says added water will result in a lighter colour than expected. A contractor can tell you on site which one applies.

### Can I just paint over a patch to hide it?

Sometimes a coating over the whole elevation is offered as a way to blend a repair, but we haven't found a published Calgary price for painting stucco, so we can't quote one. Ask the contractor what product they'd use, why, and to put it on the written quote as its own line.

### Does a colour match cost extra?

Some firms price it separately. Calgary Parging lists a colour-match add-on of $230 to $575, before GST, in its May 2026 guide, and includes colour matching in the small and medium repair lines. Other firms may fold it into the repair price, so ask for it as its own line on the written quote.
