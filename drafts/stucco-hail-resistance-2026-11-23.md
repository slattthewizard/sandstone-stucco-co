If you live in Calgary, hail is part of the weather forecast every summer, and it's reasonable to ask whether the wall on your house can take it. Stucco has a reputation for being tough. Is it earned?

## What the City of Calgary says

The City of Calgary's page on climate-ready exterior walls and siding says, in its own words: "Stucco is fire resistant and will hold up well to flying debris in moderate wind and hail, but severe storms can cause damage." The same page adds that stucco does not handle moisture well, and is not ideal for protection against heavy rain or winter storms, and that moisture and snow can cause it to shrink and crack.

That's a balanced statement. It credits stucco with standing up to ordinary hail and wind-blown debris, and it doesn't claim the wall is immune. It also flags a weakness that has nothing to do with impact: water. A hailstorm usually arrives with heavy rain, and a wall that has lost a patch of coating can take on water afterwards.

The City also says Calgary sits in what it calls "hail alley", the area that sees the most hail in Canada because of its geography, and that all communities within Calgary are equally exposed to hail. Where one storm landed doesn't make your part of the city safer or riskier next time.

Note what the City does not say. It doesn't say stucco is a certified hail-resistant material, and neither does this page. "Holds up well in moderate hail" is a plain-language assessment, not a test rating.

## What a hailstone can do to a stucco wall

Martini Stone Masonry, a Calgary contractor, describes hail impact damage as a failure it sees. It says golf-ball-sized hail, most common in July and August, leaves 2 to 5 mm indentations that break the moisture barrier. That's the firm's claim, and it's worth treating as one contractor's description, not a measured standard. But it shows the mechanism contractors worry about: the damage isn't always a dramatic hole. It can be a small dent or chip that breaks the surface and lets water in later.

That matters because dents in stucco can be hard to see from the ground. A pale chip in a dark wall is obvious. A fine pitting on a textured finish may not be, and the trouble can show up months later as staining or a bubbled patch. ## How stucco compares in big storms

Large events show what hail can do to siding in general. Environment and Climate Change Canada (ECCC), describing the June 2020 Calgary storm, wrote that hail "riddled house siding with millions of dents." For 2024, ECCC's account of the August Calgary hailstorm describes ice chunks punching holes in vinyl siding and destroying roofs. Those are the agency's words about that storm, quoted for context. They aren't a head-to-head test of stucco and vinyl, and nothing on those pages ranks one cladding against another.

The scale of the 2024 event is worth knowing. CatIQ's final industry loss estimate, published 5 August 2026, put insured losses from the August 2024 Calgary hailstorm at CAD 3.349 billion. The Insurance Bureau of Canada (IBC) reported more than 130,000 insurance claims in its February 2025 update. Our piece on [Calgary hailstorms and stucco](/blog/calgary-hailstorms-stucco/) goes through the storms and what they hit.

What you can take from this: severe hail damages many kinds of exterior. The City's wording for stucco, "severe storms can cause damage," fits that picture.

## Hail-resistant materials and your insurance

In a release dated 23 June 2026, IBC stated: "A majority of insurers will provide home insurance savings of up to 50% when hail resistant materials are in place." That's IBC's statement about insurers in general. It isn't a promise that your insurer will discount your policy, and it doesn't say stucco qualifies.

If you're curious whether your wall counts, ask your own insurer or broker directly. Good questions are:

- Does my policy offer a discount for hail-resistant materials on the walls?
- Which materials or ratings does it recognize, and is stucco one of them?
- Do I need to provide documentation, and from whom?
- Does a repair change my premium or my claim record?

Get the answers in writing if you can. The insurer's rules are what count, not a contractor's or a website's assumption.

IBC says damage from hail and wind is typically covered by home, commercial and comprehensive auto insurance, so check your own policy for the details. If you think a storm has damaged your wall, the City of Calgary advises contacting your home insurance provider before you do any repair work. IBC's steps after hail include documenting the damage with photos and keeping notes and receipts. ## Looking after a wall that has taken hail

After a storm, walk the house in daylight and look at every elevation. Photograph any chips, dents, cracks or exposed patches with a coin beside them for scale. Then check again in a week, because stains and bubbling sometimes show up after the first rain.

A few steps are generally sensible:

- Keep photos with dates, from before and after if you have them.
- Don't pressure wash the wall. Weststar Wall Systems, a Calgary firm, warns that high-pressure water can damage stucco and force moisture inside. Our guide to [pressure washing stucco](/blog/cleaning-stucco-pressure-washing/) explains a gentler approach.
- Report suspected damage to your insurer before repairs begin.
- Ask a contractor to look at any area where the coating is broken, since a breach can let water in.

Not every mark is hail. Imasco, a cement stucco manufacturer, states that some cracking and efflorescence are inherent to portland cement and are not a reflection of product quality. A contractor can help tell storm damage from ordinary ageing.

## So, is stucco good in hail?

On the public record, the answer has two halves. The City of Calgary says stucco holds up well to flying debris in moderate wind and hail, and that severe storms can cause damage. One contractor, Martini, says golf-ball-sized hail can leave small indentations that break the moisture barrier. Neither source gives a certification or a guarantee, and neither says a stucco wall is hail-proof.

For a Calgary homeowner, that suggests a practical stance: by the City's account stucco copes with ordinary hail, it can be damaged in a severe storm, and after any big event it's worth looking closely and getting a written opinion. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is stucco hail resistant?

The City of Calgary says stucco will hold up well to flying debris in moderate wind and hail, but severe storms can cause damage. That's a general assessment, not a certified rating. Ask your insurer whether it treats your wall as a hail-resistant material.

### Will my insurer give me a discount for stucco?

IBC said in June 2026 that a majority of insurers will provide home insurance savings of up to 50% when hail resistant materials are in place. That isn't a promise for any one policy. Ask your own insurer or broker what it recognizes and what documentation it needs.

### Can hail damage stucco without leaving a visible hole?

Martini Stone Masonry says golf-ball-sized hail leaves 2 to 5 mm indentations that break the moisture barrier. Small chips and dents can be easy to miss from the ground. Photograph the wall and have a contractor look at any area where the coating looks broken.

### Is stucco safer in hail than vinyl siding?

Public sources don't rank them. ECCC described ice chunks punching holes in vinyl siding in the 2024 Calgary storm, and the City says stucco holds up well in moderate hail. Neither is a side-by-side test, so treat any claim of a clear winner with caution.
