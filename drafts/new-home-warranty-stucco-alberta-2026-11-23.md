## What Alberta's new home warranty rules say

Alberta's New Home Buyer Protection Act applies only to new homes built under a building permit applied for on or after 1 February 2014. A home permitted earlier is outside the Act, though it may have other coverage you can check in your purchase papers.

For homes that are inside the Act, a home warranty insurance contract has to cover at least these periods:

| Type of defect | Minimum coverage period |
|---|---|
| Defects in materials and labour | 1 year |
| Defects in materials and labour related to delivery and distribution systems | 2 years |
| Defects in the building envelope | 5 years, from the date coverage begins |
| Structural defects | 10 years |

The Act also says a provider must offer the option to buy additional building envelope coverage, at an extra premium, for a longer prescribed period. If you bought that option, your documents should say so.

Those are minimums, so your own contract may be longer. The date that counts is when coverage begins, which isn't always the date you moved in, so read the documents for the actual start date.

## Where stucco fits: the building envelope

The Act defines the building envelope as the collection of components that separate conditioned space from unconditioned space, the exterior air or the ground. A defect in the building envelope is one that results in the failure of the building envelope to perform its intended function.

The Act does not name stucco or cladding. That means we can't tell you that a stucco problem is a covered defect, and neither can a contractor. Whether yours counts is for the warranty provider to decide. What the Act does tell you is that a newer home has at least five years of building envelope defect coverage from when coverage began, so a stucco problem in that window is worth reporting to the provider before anything else.

## What a warranty will not be

Hail damage is storm damage, not a construction defect. If a storm dented or broke your stucco, don't expect the warranty to answer for it. Hail is an insurance question, and IBC says hail damage is typically covered by home insurance policies, so check your own policy. Our guide to [hail damage on stucco in Calgary](/blog/hail-damage-stucco-calgary/) explains what to look at. This site does not handle, file or negotiate insurance claims, and a claim is between you and your insurer.

A warranty also isn't a maintenance plan, and coverage periods run out. Don't sit on a problem you've noticed. Photograph what you see now, and keep photographing it.

## Steps to take, in order

1. Find your warranty documents. Look in your purchase package from the builder or seller, or ask your lawyer or builder for a copy. IBC's list of things to gather after a loss includes "warranties" alongside proofs of purchase, photos and receipts, so keep them together.
2. Note the coverage start date and the periods that apply, including any extended envelope option.
3. Document the problem. Take dated photos from a distance and close up, with a tape measure or coin for scale. Write down when you first noticed it and what the weather was like. If you can see staining or a soft patch, photograph it and don't dig at it. Our guide to [stucco water damage warning signs](/blog/stucco-water-damage-signs/) lists what to look for.
4. Contact the warranty provider before paying for repairs. Ask how to submit a claim, what they need from you, and whether their process allows you to hire your own contractor first. Get the answer in writing if you can.
5. If the provider says the problem isn't covered, or the time has run out, get written quotes from more than one business. Alberta.ca advises that, along with a written contract and WCB coverage for the contractor and tradespeople.

Say a stucco crack opens up beside a window on a home permitted in 2018. That is a made-up example, not a customer story. Steps one to four are the same: find the papers, photograph the crack, and report it to the provider before arranging a repair. If the provider declines it, you will at least have a written answer and your documentation ready for a contractor.

## What to have ready when you call the provider

A short, factual note speeds things up. Write down your address, the date coverage began, where on the house the problem is (for example, the west wall under the second-floor window), when you first saw it, and whether it has changed since. Attach the photos. Keep a log of every call and email, with the date and the name of the person you spoke to. Don't guess at a cause in the note. Describe what you can see and let the provider and any inspector work out why.

If the problem is water getting inside, mention any staining on interior walls or ceilings as well. That is information about how the wall is behaving, and it belongs in the record even if you aren't sure the two are connected.

## Why the order matters

A contractor can tell you what they see and what they would do. They can't tell you whether the warranty provider will accept the claim. If a repair is done first, the provider may not be able to see the original condition, which is why photographs help. Don't assume either way. Ask the provider what it requires, since that is the only reliable answer.

When you hire a contractor yourself, ask about payment terms before you agree. The Prepaid Contracting rules exclude warranty-covered new homes, so a newer home with warranty coverage is a case to ask Service Alberta about rather than assume. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does my new home warranty cover stucco problems in Alberta?

It may, but it depends on the facts. The New Home Buyer Protection Act requires at least five years of coverage for building envelope defects from when coverage begins, on homes permitted on or after 1 February 2014. The Act doesn't name stucco, so whether your problem is a covered defect is for the warranty provider to decide.

### Is hail damage to stucco covered by a new home warranty?

Don't count on it. Hail damage is storm damage, not a construction defect, so don't assume the warranty applies. IBC says hail damage is typically covered by home insurance policies, so check your own policy and contact your insurer. The City of Calgary advises contacting your home insurance provider before you do any repair work.

### Should I contact the warranty provider before hiring a stucco contractor?

Yes. Report the problem and ask what the provider requires before you pay for repairs. Photograph the damage first, and keep your documents and any written answers.

### How do I know if my home is covered by the Act?

The Act applies only to new homes built under a building permit applied for on or after 1 February 2014. Your purchase documents should show whether a home warranty was issued and when coverage began. If you can't find them, ask the builder or the seller.
