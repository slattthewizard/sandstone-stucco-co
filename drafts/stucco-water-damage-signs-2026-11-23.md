# Stucco Water Damage: Warning Signs and What to Check

Stucco rarely fails all at once. Water usually gets in at a small point, spends a few winters working behind the surface, and shows itself later as a stain, a bubble or a patch that gives when you press it. By then the damage behind the wall can be larger than the mark you can see.

## Why moisture is the problem to take seriously

The City of Calgary says plainly that stucco "does not handle moisture well", and that moisture and snow can cause it to shrink and crack. A hairline crack by itself is often cosmetic. A crack that lets water in, on a wall that then freezes and thaws, is a different thing.

Calgary has plenty of those cycles. The City counted 117 freeze-thaw cycles in 2022, below the 30-year average of 128, and 54 chinook days against an average of 46. Martini Stone Masonry, a Calgary contractor, describes the mechanism this way: water gets into small cracks, freezes at -20°C and expands. That is the firm's description, but it matches what the City says about moisture and snow.

Calgary Parging, another local firm, says stucco is "designed to deflect water, not to be waterproof", and that the layer that actually keeps water out of the house is the building paper behind it. That is the contractor's claim, not a code statement, but it explains why a wall can look fine on the surface and still be wet underneath. The surface coat is only the first line.

## Warning signs you can see from the ground

Weststar Wall Systems, a Calgary contractor, lists "soft or spongy areas" and "peeling or bubbling" as warning signs of stucco damage. Those two are the most useful because you can't get them from cosmetic ageing alone. Other things to look for, none of which proves water damage by itself:

- Stains or dark streaks, especially below windows, under rooflines or at the base of a wall.
- A bulge or a section that looks as if it has pulled away from the wall.
- Cracks that widen from one year to the next, or that run in a line from the corner of a window.
- Crumbling, powdery or flaking surface in one area while the rest of the wall is sound.
- Gaps where stucco meets a window or door frame. Martini says temperature movement can open 3 to 8 mm gaps there.
- Staining, mould or a musty smell on the inside wall below the same spot.

Not every mark is a leak. Some cracking and efflorescence, the white powdery deposit on cement, are normal. Imasco, a cement stucco manufacturer, states that some cracking and efflorescence "are inherent to portland cement, and are not a reflection of product quality." The point is to notice what changes. A mark that stays the same for years is a different matter from one that grows, darkens or softens.

## A self-check you can do in ten minutes

You don't need a ladder and you shouldn't use one near a damaged wall. Walk the house with a phone and a notebook, ideally a day or two after rain or snowmelt.

1. Start at the roofline. Look for gutters that are sagging, overflowing or pulling away, and for downspouts that empty against the wall instead of away from it.
2. Look at every window and door. Check the edges of the frame for gaps, and the stucco below each sill for stains.
3. Check the lowest strip of the wall. Martini says snow piled against stucco softens and crumbles the lowest 12 to 18 inches, so look there for crumbling, and clear any drift or ice that is stacked against it.
4. Press gently on any suspicious patch with your palm. A firm wall doesn't move. A soft or spongy spot is one of the signs Weststar lists, so photograph it and don't dig at it.
5. Tap lightly with a knuckle around the patch. A hollow sound can mean the coat has lost its bond, which a contractor can confirm on site.
6. Go indoors and check the matching spot on the inside wall and the ceiling below any upper-floor window.
7. Photograph everything with a tape measure or coin beside it, and write the date down. Repeat in a few months.

Don't pressure wash to see what is underneath. Weststar warns that high-pressure water can damage stucco and force moisture inside it, and Imasco's specification says not to spray walls with high-pressure water for at least 72 hours after application. Don't seal a suspicious spot with caulk or paint either, because that can trap water behind it.

## Where the water usually gets in

Contractors point to a handful of entry points, and several come back to the roofline and the openings. Calgary Parging said in 2024 that over 30% of its residential repairs address water damage caused by incorrectly installed rain gutters, eaves or flashing. That figure comes from one firm's own jobs and is not a city-wide statistic, but it is a fair reason to start your check at the gutters. Our guide to [stucco leaks at gutters, flashing and roof lines](/blog/stucco-gutter-flashing-leaks/) goes through those points one at a time.

Other routes include cracks that have opened over time, failed seals and gaps around windows and doors, and holes made by birds or by someone fixing something to the wall. If a woodpecker or flicker has made a hole, see our page on [hail, woodpecker and stucco damage](/hail-woodpecker-stucco-damage/), because an open hole lets water in too. Flickers are federally protected, so don't harm a bird or disturb a nest.

Hail is a separate cause, and the dents and breaks it makes can also let water in. That is covered in the [hail damage guide](/blog/hail-damage-stucco-calgary/) and not repeated here.

## Why nobody can price the damage from the ground

Water damage is hard to size from photographs. Calgary Stucco and Slate says, "Nobody can tell you the full extent of water damage until the wall is opened up." That is one firm's statement, but it is the honest answer to the first question most owners ask.

What a contractor will usually do is look at the wall, tap and probe suspect areas, and then price on what they find. Opening the wall may show sound sheathing and a repair that is mostly cosmetic. It may also show sheathing that has to be replaced first. Ask for the quote to say how the contractor will find out, and what happens to the price if the opened wall turns out worse than expected.

## Repair or replace: how contractors frame it

Total Exteriors, a Calgary firm, sets out a simple test. It suggests repair when the damage is localized, moisture intrusion is minimal and hasn't spread, and the majority of the stucco is structurally sound. It suggests replacement when damage is widespread or structural, or moisture problems are extensive. That is one contractor's rule of thumb, and a quote should explain why it chose one path over the other.

Whichever way the quote goes, ask what is being done about the cause. A new coat over a wall that is still taking in water from a bad downspout will probably end up in the same condition. ## Three example situations

These are hypotheticals, not customer stories.

Say a south-facing wall has a bubbled patch about the size of a dinner plate beneath a window, and the paint is peeling. That matches two of the signs Weststar lists. It would be worth a contractor looking at the window frame edge and the sill before anyone prices a patch.

Say a back corner has a dark stain that runs down from the roofline, and the downspout there empties against the wall. The stain and the downspout point to the gutter-and-flashing route, so the first question is what is happening at the roof edge, not only what is on the wall.

Say a lower strip near a driveway is crumbling where snow gets shovelled against it every winter. That matches what Martini describes about snow piled against stucco. The repair and the habit of piling snow there both need attention.

## If the damage might involve insurance or a warranty

If a hailstorm or another insured event may be behind the damage, the City of Calgary advises contacting your home insurance provider before you do any repair work. The Insurance Bureau of Canada suggests documenting the damage and says taking photos can be helpful. This site does not handle, file or negotiate insurance claims. A contractor can give you a written repair estimate that you may pass to your insurer, and the claim is between you and your insurer.

If your home is newer, check your new home warranty before paying for repairs. Whether a particular water problem counts as a covered defect is for the warranty provider to decide, so don't assume it does.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does stucco water damage look like?

Weststar Wall Systems lists soft or spongy areas and peeling or bubbling as warning signs. Stains, bulges, crumbling patches, widening cracks and gaps at window frames are other things to watch. None of them proves a leak on its own, so photograph the spot and have a contractor look at it.

### Can I tell how bad the damage is just by looking?

Not reliably. Calgary Stucco and Slate says nobody can tell the full extent of water damage until the wall is opened up. A written quote should explain how the contractor will find out and how the price changes if the wall is worse than expected.

### How much does it cost to fix water-damaged stucco in Calgary?

There is no single price. Calgary Parging says water-damaged sheathing or board must be replaced first and adds $575 to $2,300 or more, before GST, to the repair. Dynamic Painting Inc. lists moisture-related or larger corrective work at $3,500 to $8,500 or more, GST not stated. Those are two firms' figures, and the contractor who sees your wall sets the actual price.

### Should I seal or paint over a suspicious patch?

It is better to leave it alone and get it looked at. Caulk or paint over a damaged spot can trap moisture behind it, and pressure washing can force water inside the wall, as Weststar warns. Document the patch and ask a contractor to check it.
