# Do You Need a Stucco Permit in Calgary?

Most owners ask about permits right after they get a quote, and it is a fair question to put to the contractor and to the City. The short version from the City of Calgary's own pages is that most repair work needs no permit, but changing the type of cladding or the size of your windows does. The details matter, and so does a phone call before work starts.

## What the City of Calgary says about repairs

The City's storm recovery page says that for most repair or replacement work you will not need a development or building permit. It does not list a permit for siding and trim in the same material. It also lists no permit for roofing materials, a door, window or skylight of the same size, or eavestroughs and downspouts.

A second City page, on home renovations, says replacements are allowed when you maintain and repair building components, as long as you replace the component with a similar material or part that performs as well as, or better than, what you are replacing. It names exterior cladding, windows, doors and mechanical systems. The same page says all changes must also meet the applicable National Building Code (Alberta Edition) requirements.

Neither page uses the word stucco. The City's pages talk about siding and cladding in general terms, so a call to 311 is the way to be sure the City reads your particular job the same way.

## A plain summary of when a permit is likely

Going by the City's pages, this is where the lines fall:

| What you are doing | What the City's pages say |
|---|---|
| Repairing or replacing stucco with the same material | Generally no City permit |
| Changing to a different cladding material | A building permit |
| Making windows or doors larger or smaller | Development and building permits |
| Creating new openings in exterior walls | A permit is needed |

Treat that table as a starting point for a conversation with 311, not as a ruling on your house. Whether re-cladding alone needs a development permit in a given area, such as a heritage district, is not something this site can tell you, so ask the City's Planning Services Centre or 311.

## The Alberta Permit Regulation, in plain terms

Behind the City's guidance sits a provincial rule. Alberta's Permit Regulation under the Safety Codes Act says a building permit is required for the construction of a building, including a renovation of or addition to a building. It then lists exemptions. Two are worth knowing about when you are thinking about an exterior job.

The first covers construction, renovation or addition work that does not exceed $5,000 in prevailing market value, as long as matters affecting health or safety are not at risk. The second covers re-roofing or re-siding done for aesthetic purposes or to maintain the building, where the new material is of a similar type, health and safety are not at risk, and there is no structural change to the building.

Here is the catch. The regulation does not say whether "siding" includes stucco, and we have not found an official answer. Do not read the second exemption as a statement that stucco work is exempt. It is simply one of the places the provincial rule talks about replacing cladding with something similar. The City's answer for your project is the one to ask for.

## When the answer is more likely to be yes

Some jobs are more than a repair. These are the kinds of changes the City's pages single out:

- Switching the exterior to a different material. A building permit is listed for siding with a different material, so if you are thinking of going from stucco to another cladding, ask 311 before you sign.
- Changing window or door sizes. Larger or smaller windows or doors need development and building permits.
- Adding a new opening in an exterior wall.

Say a homeowner wants to remove a damaged stucco wall and replace it with the same type of stucco: that matches the like-for-like language. Say a different homeowner wants to put in a bigger window while the wall is open: that is a changed opening, and the City lists development and building permits for it. These are made-up examples to show where the lines fall, not stories about real customers.

## Who applies for a permit when one is needed

That is for you and your contractor to settle in writing before work starts. Ask the contractor whether they think a permit applies, who will apply, and what happens to the schedule and the price if one does. A contractor who says "no permit needed" should be able to explain why, and you can check it with 311 yourself. Keep a note of the date, the name of whoever you spoke to and what they said.

If the work is covered by an insurance claim, the City of Calgary advises contacting your home insurance provider before you do any repair work. This site does not handle, file or negotiate insurance claims, and the claim is between you and your insurer.

## Checking the contractor, not just the permit

A permit is about the work. The business is a separate check. The City of Calgary says contractors are required to have a valid City of Calgary business licence, and you can call 311 to confirm that a contractor has one. That is the same phone number, so you can ask both questions in one call.

Alberta.ca advises homeowners to insist on a written contract, to get written estimates from more than one business, and to make sure the contractor and tradespeople have Workers' Compensation Board (WCB) coverage. Our guides on [how to hire a stucco contractor in Calgary](/blog/hiring-stucco-contractor-calgary/) and on [deposits and contracts in Alberta](/blog/stucco-contract-deposit-alberta/) cover those points in detail.

Two further notes. A contractor who takes money before the work is finished, and who solicits, negotiates or concludes the contract in person away from its own premises, needs an Alberta prepaid contracting licence, and you can check it with Service Alberta. And stucco is not a compulsory-certification trade in Alberta, so there is no trade certificate to look for the way there is with electricians or gasfitters. What you can check is the business licence, the prepaid contracting licence where it applies, WCB coverage and the written contract.

## A short list to take to 311

Before you accept a quote, it helps to have the following ready when you call:

- Your address and which walls are involved.
- Whether the work is a repair or a full re-stucco, and whether it is the same material.
- Whether any windows, doors or openings change.
- Whether the work is related to a recent storm, since the City has a page on storm damage.
- The contractor's business name, so you can ask 311 to confirm the business licence.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Do I need a permit to repair stucco in Calgary?

The City of Calgary says most repair or replacement work does not need a development or building permit, and it lists no permit for siding and trim in the same material. Stucco is not named on that page, so check your own project with 311 before work starts.

### Do I need a permit to change my stucco to a different material?

The City lists a building permit for siding with a different material. If you are thinking about moving from stucco to something else, ask 311 whether your plan counts as a different material and what permits apply.

### Does the $5,000 rule in Alberta mean small stucco jobs never need a permit?

Not necessarily. The Permit Regulation exempts some construction, renovation or addition work up to $5,000 in prevailing market value, but only if matters affecting health or safety are not at risk. Whether and how that applies to a stucco job is for the City to say, so confirm it with 311 and don't rely on the figure alone.

### How do I check that a Calgary stucco contractor has a business licence?

The City says contractors are required to have a valid City of Calgary business licence, and you can call 311 to confirm one. Have the business name ready. Also ask about WCB coverage and get the contract in writing.
