## 0. Legal framing (Alberta)

### 0.a Consumer Protection Act (CPA) + prepaid contracting

**Versions read:** Consumer Protection Act, RSA 2000 c C-26.3, "Current as of July 14, 2026" [L1]. Designation of
Trades and Businesses Regulation, AR 178/99, "Current as of May 23, 2024" [L2]. Prepaid Contracting Business
Licensing Regulation, AR 185/99, "Current as of June 15, 2026" (expires "June 30, 2028", s 14) [L3].

**Who needs the prepaid contracting licence.** CPA s 104(1): "No person may engage in a designated business unless the
person holds a licence under this Act that authorizes the person to engage in that business." [L1]
Prepaid contracting is designated by AR 178/99 s 5(1): "Part 10 of the Consumer Protection Act applies to the prepaid
contracting business." [L2] Definitions, s 5(2) [L2], verbatim:

- "(a) "construction or maintenance contract" means a contract for the purpose of (i) constructing, altering,
  maintaining, repairing, adding to or improving (A) a building that is used or is to be used by the owner, occupier
  or person in control of it as the owner's, occupier's or person's own private dwelling, or (B) a structure that is
  to be used in connection with a building referred to in paragraph (A) and that is located on the same parcel as
  that building, or (ii) altering, maintaining or improving real property to be used in connection with a building
  or structure referred to in subclause (i), but does not include a contract referred to in subsection (3);"
- "(b) "prepaid contract" means a construction or maintenance contract in which all or part of the contract price is
  to be paid before all the goods or services called for in the contract are provided;"
- "(c) "prepaid contracting business" means the activities of soliciting, negotiating or concluding in person, at any
  place other than the seller's place of business, a prepaid contract."
- s 5(3) excludes business-use buildings, contracts between contractors and subtrades, warranty-covered new homes
  and water wells. [L2]

**Does it cover stucco / exterior renovation?** The word "stucco" does not appear in s 5. Repairing or altering the
exterior of an owner-occupied private dwelling falls within "constructing, altering, maintaining, repairing, adding
to or improving ... a building that is used ... as the owner's ... own private dwelling" [L2]. Alberta.ca describes
covered work as "Construction, maintenance, repairing, altering, adding to or improving private dwellings, or real
property used in conjunction with a private dwelling such as landscaping services." [L4] **Our reading:** a stucco
contractor who takes a deposit and solicits, negotiates or concludes that contract in person away from its own
premises needs the licence.

**Licence facts (AR 185/99)** [L3]: fee "$78" (s 7(1)); security required, "in a form and in an amount approved by the
Director" (s 5(1)); the amount is NOT CONFIRMED (not stated in the regulation or on [L4]). s 10(1)-(2): for prepaid
contracts "more than $200" the contract must comply with CPA s 35 and set out "quality or types of materials to be
used under the contract and the services and work to be carried out". s 8.1(1) bars soliciting at the home for
"(a) furnaces; (b) air conditioners; (c) water heaters; (d) windows; (e) energy audits" unless invited. **Stucco and
siding are not on that list.** [L3]

**Does anything bind a party that only advertises or refers leads?**

- Prepaid licence: **our reading, no.** The designated activity is "soliciting, negotiating or concluding **in
  person** ... a prepaid contract" [L2]. The site does not meet anyone in person, is not a party to the contract and
  takes no part of the contract price.
- **But the CPA's general rules probably DO bind the site.** CPA s 1(1)(l): "supplier" means "a person who, in the
  course of the person's business, (i) provides goods or services to consumers, (ii) manufactures, assembles or
  produces goods, **(iii) promotes the use or purchase of goods or services**, or **(iv) receives or is entitled to
  receive money or other consideration as a result of the provision of goods or services to consumers**, and includes
  any salesperson, employee, representative or agent of the person" [L1]. **Our reading:** a lead-gen site paid by
  contractors fits (iii) and likely (iv), so s 6 (unfair practices) applies to what the site says.
- "services" includes "a service offered or provided that involves the addition to or maintenance, repair or
  alteration of goods or any residential dwelling" (s 1(1)(k)(i)). [L1]
- s 1(1)(a.1): "advertiser" means "a person who prints, publishes, distributes, broadcasts or telecasts other
  people's advertisements". s 9(1): "No advertiser may print, publish, distribute, broadcast or telecast a supplier's
  advertisement for goods or a service if the advertisement contains an unfair practice." s 9(2) exempts an advertiser
  acting "in good faith and in the ordinary course of business." [L1] (If we write the copy ourselves, rely on s 6,
  not the s 9(2) good-faith shelter.)

**Unfair practices that bind a supplier (s 6)** [L1], verbatim extracts:

- s 6(1.1): "It is an offence for a supplier to engage in an unfair practice."
- s 6(2)(c): "to use exaggeration, innuendo or ambiguity as to a material fact with respect to the consumer
  transaction;"
- s 6(4)(a): "a supplier's doing or saying anything that might reasonably deceive or mislead a consumer;"
- s 6(4)(d): "a supplier's representation that the supplier has a sponsorship, approval, status, qualification,
  affiliation or connection that the supplier does not have;"
- s 6(4)(k): "a supplier's representation that the supplier can supply goods or services if the supplier cannot;"
- s 6(4)(p): "a supplier's representation that a part, replacement, repair or adjustment is needed or desirable if it
  is not;"
- s 6(4)(x): a representation "about the performance, capability or length of life of goods or services unless (i)
  the representation is based on adequate and proper independent testing" (plus (ii)-(iii): the testing substantiates
  it and it is fairly reflected)
- s 6(4)(z): "a supplier's representation that appears in an objective form such as an editorial, documentary or
  scientific report when the representation is primarily made to sell goods or services, unless the representation
  states that it is an advertisement or promotion;"
- s 8: "An unfair practice may occur before, during or after a consumer transaction, and is an unfair practice for
  all the purposes of this Part even if no consumer transaction is entered into or concluded."

**Direct sales (Part 2)** [L1]: s 24(a.1) "direct sales contract" is a consumer contract where "(i) the consideration
... exceeds an amount specified in the regulations, and (ii) the contract is negotiated or concluded in person at a
place other than the supplier's place of business" (market/fair exceptions). s 27: "A consumer may, without any
reason, cancel a direct sales contract at any time from the date the sales contract is entered into until, subject
to the regulations, 10 days after the consumer receives a copy of the written sales contract." s 28(2)(a): one-year
cancellation right "if the supplier was required to be licensed under Part 10 and was not licensed at the time the
direct sales contract was concluded". The dollar threshold in the regulations: NOT CONFIRMED.

**Alberta.ca consumer advice** [L5]: "Most problems relating to home renovations involve contractors who solicit
contracts door-to-door." "Always insist on a written contract." "Get written estimates from more than one business."
"Make sure the contractor and tradespeople have Workers' Compensation Board (WCB) coverage."

### 0.b Insurance Act (Alberta): adjusters

**Versions read:** Insurance Act, RSA 2000 c I-3, "Current as of May 14, 2026" [L6]; Insurance Agents and Adjusters
Regulation, AR 122/2001, "Current as of July 30, 2026" [L7].

**Definition, s 2(1)** [L6]: ""adjuster" means a person who, for compensation, (a) directly or indirectly solicits the
right to negotiate the settlement of a loss under a contract of insurance on behalf of an insured or insurer or a
reciprocal insurance exchange, or (b) negotiates the settlement of a loss under a contract of insurance on behalf of
an insured or insurer or a reciprocal insurance exchange."

s 2(2) excludes a lawyer, "an agent of an owner of or person having an insurable interest in property who negotiates
a settlement of a loss ... in respect of the property", an insurer, and an insured's employee [L6]. (Do not rely on
the owner's-agent exclusion: the site is not the owner's agent and must not become one.)

**Licence rule, s 460** [L6]: "(1) No business may act or offer to act as an adjuster in respect of a contract of
insurance unless (a) the business holds a valid and subsisting adjuster's certificate of authority, or (b) [an
insurance agent that sold the policy, for losses under the prescribed amount]." s 460(2): "No individual may act or
offer to act as an adjuster in respect of a contract of insurance unless" one of four conditions is met (licensed
adjuster working for a licensed business; qualifying agent; insurer's employee; reciprocal exchange attorney).

AR 122/2001 s 19(2) sets level 1, 2 and 3 adjuster certificates [L7]. The Alberta Insurance Council also issues a "Restricted Hail Adjuster" licence [L8]. "Public adjuster"
is not a defined term in the Act (searched the text; not found) [L6].

**Conclusion for the site (our reading of s 2(1) and s 460):** "offer to act" and "directly or indirectly solicits the
right to negotiate" are both caught, and the site is paid by contractors ("for compensation"). So:

- MAY: explain the claim process in general terms using IBC's own steps (section 2.3), tell owners to call their
  insurer first (Calgary: "Before you do any repair work on your property, contact your home insurance provider." [L13]),
  and say contractors can provide a written repair estimate the owner may give to the insurer.
- MUST NEVER: offer to handle, manage, negotiate, file, "fight", "maximize" or "get approved" a claim; say we or our
  contractors "work with your adjuster for you", "deal with the insurance company", or "negotiate your settlement";
  promise a claim outcome; describe any partner as a (public) adjuster unless the AIC licence was checked.

**No.** Tradesecrets (Government of Alberta) lists the compulsory certification trades [L9]: Appliance Service

Operator, Electrician, Elevator Constructor, Gasfitter, Hairstylist, Heavy Equipment Technician, Ironworker,

"Lather (Interior Systems Mechanic)" is on the **optional** list [L10]; its profile says "Optional Certification" and

as an apprentice and work under the supervision of a qualified individual, be a certified journeyperson, hold a
recognized trade certificate, or have an employer that is satisfied you have the skills and knowledge expected of
certified journeyperson." No trade named "plasterer" or "stucco" appears on either list [L9][L10]. The trade
regulation text itself (King's Printer) was NOT read; this answer rests on the Tradesecrets pages.

### 1.3 New Home Buyer Protection Act (warranty on newer homes)

New Home Buyer Protection Act, SA 2012 c N-3.2, "Current as of December 1, 2025" [L15]. s 2: "This Act applies only to
new homes constructed under a building permit applied for on or after February 1, 2014." s 3(6): a home warranty
insurance contract must cover "(a) defects in materials and labour for a period of at least one year ..., (b) defects
in materials and labour related to delivery and distribution systems for a period of at least 2 years ..., (c)
defects in the building envelope for a period of at least 5 years starting on the date on which the coverage begins,
and (d) structural defects for a period of at least 10 years". s 3(7): a provider "must offer the option to purchase,
at an additional premium, additional coverage covering (a) defects in the building envelope for a prescribed period".
[L15]

s 1: ""building envelope" means the collection of components that separate conditioned space from unconditioned
space, the exterior air or the ground"; ""defects in the building envelope" means defects that result in the failure
of the building envelope to perform its intended function". [L15]

What this means for stucco (our reading, keep it soft): a Calgary home built under a permit applied for on or after
1 Feb 2014 has **at least** 5 years of building-envelope defect coverage from when coverage began. The Act does not
name stucco or cladding; whether a given stucco problem is a covered "defect" is for the warranty provider. Site copy:
"If your home is newer, check your new home warranty before paying for repairs." Hail damage is not a defect: do not
imply the warranty covers it.

## 3. Woodpeckers (northern flicker)

- **Protected: yes.** The Convention (Schedule to the Migratory Birds Convention Act, 1994, current to 2026-09-21)
  lists as "Migratory Insectivorous Birds": "Bobolinks, catbirds, chickadees, cuckoos, **flickers**, flycatchers, ...
  **woodpeckers**, and wrens" [L30]; the amended list includes "Picidae (woodpeckers and allies)" [L30]. Canada.ca's
  list of birds protected under the MBCA includes "Northern Flicker | Pic flamboyant | Picidae | Colaptes auratus"
  [L32].
- Migratory Birds Regulations, 2022 (SOR/2022-105), s 5(1) [L31]: "A person must not engage in any of the following
  activities unless they have a permit that authorizes them to do so or they are authorized by these Regulations to
  do so: (a) capture, kill, take, injure or harass a migratory bird or attempt to do so; (b) destroy, take or disturb
  an egg; and (c) damage, destroy, remove or disturb a nest, nest shelter, eider duck shelter or duck box."
- s 5(2)(b): may be removed without a permit: "a nest that was built by a species that is not listed in a Table to
  Schedule 1 if that nest does not contain a live bird or a viable egg" [L31]. Schedule 1's only woodpecker is
  "Dryocopus pileatus Pileated Woodpecker" (36 months); **the northern flicker is not in Schedule 1** [L31].
- s 63(1): "Despite the prohibition on harassing a migratory bird set out in paragraph 5(1)(a), a person may, without a
  permit, use equipment other than an aircraft or firearms to scare migratory birds that are causing or are likely to
  cause danger to human health or public safety or damage to agricultural, environmental or other interests." [L31]
- ECCC FAQ [L33]: "It is prohibited to damage, destroy, disturb or remove the nest of a migratory bird that contains a
  live bird or viable egg." Deterrence: "Measures may be taken to discourage the bird from building another nest in
  the same location, as long as the measure does not disturb or cause harm to the bird. For instance, you may cut off
  access to that part of the machinery by covering it with a tarp or wire mesh".

**What the site may say:** flickers are federally protected; do not kill, trap or harm them, and do not disturb a hole
with eggs or chicks in it; an empty flicker hole can be repaired; non-harmful scare devices and covering the area
(e.g. mesh) are allowed; repair the stucco after nesting ends. Anything beyond that needs a federal damage or danger
permit. Flicker damage to stucco as a Calgary-specific fact: NOT CONFIRMED (no primary source found).

## P5. Technical facts

### P5.1 Systems on Calgary houses
- "Cement stucco with an acrylic finish continues to be the main go-to stucco product for over 87% of residential
  properties . The top brands we install are Adex Systems Inc., Sto Corp, and Dryvit" - one Calgary contractor's own
  job mix, 2024, not a city survey. [P6]
- Martini names the systems it meets: "conventional three-coat, IMASCO or EIFS". [P2]
- Traditional 3-coat (Imasco guide spec, Revision 05, 22 Oct 2024) [P31]: scratch coat "to a minimum thickness of 10 mm
  (3/8")"; brown coat "to a maximum thickness of 10mm"; "Second (brown) coat application to bring the combined total
  system thickness to 19 mm". Imasco is a Surrey, BC manufacturer [P32].
- "Stucco is designed to deflect water, not to be waterproof. The layer that actually keeps water out of your home is
  the building paper behind it." (contractor claim, Calgary Parging) [P4]
- Acrylic vs cement finish (contractor claim, Martini): acrylic "stays flexible through freeze-thaw, holds colour
  without repainting and resists hairline cracking better than cement finish." [P1]

### P5.2 Failure modes (contractor claims; attribute, do not state as fact)
- Martini [P2], verbatim: "Freeze-thaw cracking: water penetrates small cracks, freezes at -20°C and expands";
  "Hail impact damage: golf-ball-sized hail (most common in July and August) leaves 2-5 mm indentations that break the
  moisture barrier"; "snow piled against stucco softens and crumbles the lowest 12-18 inches of the wall"; "Window and
  door separation: temperature movement opens 3-8 mm gaps where stucco meets frames."
- Calgary Parging [P4]: "Flickers work Calgary stucco hard, especially on west and south elevations in spring." [P5]:
  woodpeckers are drawn to EIFS because "the foam insulation behind the finish is much softer than concrete, and
  tapping on it produces the hollow resonance" birds associate with insects.
- Calgary Parging [P6]: "Over 30% of residential repairs address water damage caused by incorrectly installed rain
  gutters, eaves, or flashing" (own jobs, 2024).
- Weststar [P11]: warning signs "Soft or spongy areas", "Peeling or bubbling"; "High-pressure water can damage stucco
  and force moisture inside."
- Calgary Stucco and Slate [P22]: "Nobody can tell you the full extent of water damage until the wall is opened up."
- Imasco TDS [P33]: "Some cracking and efflorescence are inherent to portland cement, and are not a reflection of
  product quality." (manufacturer)
- Hail loss figure: Total Exteriors says the August 2024 Calgary hailstorm "reached about $3.25 billion" insured
  [P10]. Verified at merge against IBC 11 Feb 2025 [L19]; cite IBC, not the contractor.

### P5.3 Repair vs re-stucco
- Total Exteriors [P10]: repair when "The damage is localized", "Moisture intrusion is minimal and hasn't spread",
  "The majority of your stucco is structurally sound"; replace when "Damage is widespread or structural", "Moisture
  problems are extensive", or "The stucco is beyond its natural lifespan (40–50 years)".
- High End Construction [P12]: "Large cracks (over 1/4″ wide) or stucco that is crumbling off the wall are signs of
  deeper issues". Lifespans conflict between firms: 40-50 years [P10], "20–40 years" (Calgary Parging, extractor) [P3].

### P5.4 Curing, temperature, timing (manufacturer data = firm; contractor claims = attribute)
- **Imasco traditional stucco** (guide spec 2024) [P31]: "perform no stucco work when the ambient air and substrate
  temperatures are below 4°C (39°F) or above 38°C (100°F) for 24 hours before, during and after stucco application."
  "Moist cure cement based stucco with clean water to maintain plaster uniformly moist for a minimum of 48 hours".
  "Do not spray walls with high pressure water for at least 72 hours." Finish coat: substrate "above 4°C (39°F) for a
  minimum of 24 hours during and after application".
- **Imasco cement finish TDS** (9/24/2024) [P33]: "Finish coat may be applied after a minimum of 7 days, during ideal
  conditions." Basecoat "should be applied above 4°C (39°F) and should remain above 4°C (39°F) for a minimum of 48
  hours."
- **ADEX DRYMIX base coat**, Technical Bulletin #1.2, May 2021 [P34]: "Ambient and surface temperatures must be kept at
  or above 5 °C (41 °F) for a minimum of 24 hours." Curing "Approximately 24 hours under normal climatic conditions."
  Additives "(antifreeze, accelerators, or otherwise) are strictly forbidden and may void warranties."
- **ADEX CRYSTAL liquid coat** (acrylic), Technical Bulletin #4.5, August 2019 [P35]: "Ambient and surface temperatures
  must be at or above 5 °C (41 °F) for a minimum of 24 hours." "All cement-based substrates must have a mandatory
  1-month curing process before CRYSTAL liquid coat is applied." Dry to touch "after 3 hours" at 21°C/50% RH,
  "completely dry after 12 hours". (A liquid coat over a finish, not ADEX's trowelled acrylic finish itself.)
- Contractor claims: Calgary Parging season "April through October" [P3]; Martini "Most residential repairs take 3-7
  days ... Minor crack repairs finish in 1-2 days" and "Winter repairs proceed when temperatures hold above -5°C for 48
  hours, using heated enclosures and IMASCO cold-weather additives" [P2]. **CONFLICT:** -5°C is below every
  manufacturer minimum above (4-5°C at the wall); do not repeat it. Calgary Stucco and Slate: "Some repairs take two
  trips; some take up to four, with up to weeks in between so each layer cures properly." [P22]

### P5.5 Why colour-matching a patch is hard
- Imasco colours datasheet (21 March 2018) [P32]: "Variations in colour may occur due to weather, texture, job
  conditions and method of application." "It is important to purchase the same colour batch number for the complete
  job." Adding water while floating or trowelling "will disturb the colour".
- Imasco TDS [P33]: variations "are more noticeable on in darker colors" (sic); "the addition of water will result in
  a lighter color than expected."
- Imasco guide spec [P31]: "Avoid application of separate batches of finish side-by-side".
- Calgary Parging [P4]: "a cement finish and an acrylic finish age and weather differently. ... On heavily weathered or
  discontinued finishes we'll say upfront if a perfect match isn't achievable, and give you the option of refinishing
  a full elevation instead of chasing an invisible patch." Martini [P1]: "Matching an aged finish takes trial batches."
